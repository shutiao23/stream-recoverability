## Drop-in caption

**Figure 2.** Heterogeneity of the screening gain. **(a)** Which endpoints keep the gain. For each of five endpoints, the difference in AUC between the better timing axis (peak-window overlap or warm-season coverage, whichever scores higher) and the outage-length rule, on the v17 development panel (40,704 cases in 110 networks, identical for every endpoint). Points are the contrasts, bars the network-clustered 95% intervals (1200 clustered bootstrap draws, resampling networks). Positive values mean the timing axis separates consequential errors better than outage length; negative values mean the length rule wins. Endpoints are ordered most positive to most negative: hottest 7-day mean +0.072 (+0.063 to +0.096); hottest 30-day mean +0.070 (+0.061 to +0.098); amplitude +0.024 (+0.015 to +0.037); annual mean -0.027 (-0.036 to -0.018); coldest 30-day mean -0.184 (-0.227 to -0.156). The two warm extremes keep the gain, the amplitude is weakly positive, and the ordering reverses at the annual mean: the annual mean and the cold extreme are the two endpoints where the outage-length rule is the better axis. Each endpoint is scored at its own natural-scale tolerance (0.5 °C divided by 1.062 times the endpoint's interannual standard deviation): hottest 7-day mean 0.54 °C; hottest 30-day mean 0.51 °C; amplitude 0.64 °C; annual mean 0.26 °C; coldest 30-day mean 0.38 °C. The interval excludes zero on both sides of the reversal, so the sign of the endpoint contrast is resolved, not marginal.

**(b)** Where the gain holds, and where it collapses. Forest plot of the pooled contrast between the certificate screen and the published-style outage-length rule, certificate rate minus length-rule rate in percentage points of 'consequential error' (hottest 30-day mean daily water temperature wrong by more than 0.5 °C), at 70% retention, so negative values favour the screen. Bars are network-clustered 95% intervals; the solid line is zero and the dashed line is the pre-declared threshold Δ = -1.0 percentage point, below which transport of the screen is declared supported. Panels are shown oldest to newest and are three kinds of evidence, distinguished by colour and by the legend inside the panel: grey, retrospective (v17 development and the v17 reserved holdout, both scored on designed masks over complete years); blue, sealed confirmation (the never-used USGS panel, scored once, with its empirical arm and a post-seal clean-subset diagnostic); orange, transport (a pre-registered multi-agency panel designed after the USGS score was known). The pooled retrospective panels give -6.35 points [-7.12, -5.52] on development and -7.13 points [-8.97, -5.36] on the reserved holdout; sealed confirmation gives -7.94 points [-10.15, -5.75] on the pooled panel, its empirical arm -1.74 points [-2.98, -0.68] (the weakest cell of the figure: it is the one estimate that does not clear the threshold) and the clean subsets -9.42 points [-12.44, -6.03] on the networks with no outcome-bearing prior use; transport gives -4.61 points [-6.53, -3.30] on the primary panel (32 networks across three agencies), -4.35 [-6.28, -3.15] for SMHI, -6.40 [-7.97, +1.29] for UK EA (2 networks; the only interval of the ten that reaches above zero, and it does not reach the threshold either), -4.89 [-8.51, -1.47] for Hub'Eau, and -6.14 [-8.70, -4.59] on the NVE secondary stratum, whose provider appears in development and which is therefore never part of the primary panel. Nine of ten estimates lie below the pre-declared threshold; the exception is the sealed empirical arm (-1.74 points, interval [-2.98, -0.68]), which is the arm where real outage geometry replaces the designed masks. Every interval is a 95% network-clustered confidence interval for the difference in consequential-error rates, in percentage points; intervals that exclude zero are the ones that resolve the direction of the contrast. The right-hand column gives the cases and networks behind each row.

## What is not in the panel

- **real-archive empirical arm** — not plotted. The real archive is the 2422 real incomplete station-years of e2_archive_deployment. Those years carry no truth by construction, so no certificate-minus-length error-rate contrast (or interval) exists for them; the requested row cannot be sourced and is not drawn.
  The closest outcome-agnostic evidence for the real archive is the admission disagreement between the two rules, which needs no truth: on 2422 real incomplete station-years the incumbent unfilled limit admits 83.2% and the peak-window rule 81.2%, and the two disagree on 15.1% of them (`results/v18_closure/e2_archive_deployment/threshold_disagreement.csv`, row 'Johnson unfilled, longest run <= 49 d'). That is a rate of disagreement, not an error-rate contrast, and it has no place on this axis.
