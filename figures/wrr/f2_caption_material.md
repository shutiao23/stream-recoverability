## Drop-in caption

**Figure 2.** Heterogeneity of the screening gain. **(a)** Which endpoints keep the gain. For each of five endpoints, the difference in AUC between the better timing axis (peak-window overlap or warm-season coverage, whichever scores higher) and the outage-length rule, on the v17 development panel (40,704 cases in 110 networks, identical for every endpoint). Points are the contrasts, bars the network-clustered 95% intervals (1200 clustered bootstrap draws, resampling networks). Positive values mean the timing axis separates consequential errors better than outage length; negative values mean the length rule wins. Endpoints are ordered most positive to most negative: hottest 7-day mean +0.072 (+0.063 to +0.096); hottest 30-day mean +0.070 (+0.061 to +0.098); amplitude +0.024 (+0.015 to +0.037); annual mean -0.027 (-0.036 to -0.018); coldest 30-day mean -0.184 (-0.227 to -0.156). The two warm extremes keep the gain, the amplitude is weakly positive, and the ordering reverses at the annual mean. The cold-extreme negative contrast uses a warm-season support axis and is not evidence that length governs winter extrema. Each endpoint is scored at its own natural-scale tolerance (0.5 °C divided by 1.062 times the endpoint's interannual standard deviation): hottest 7-day mean 0.54 °C; hottest 30-day mean 0.51 °C; amplitude 0.64 °C; annual mean 0.26 °C; coldest 30-day mean 0.38 °C. The interval excludes zero on both sides of the reversal, so the sign of the endpoint contrast is resolved, not marginal.

**(b)** Where the gain holds across evidence panels. Forest plot of the pooled contrast between the certificate screen and the published-style outage-length rule, certificate rate minus length-rule rate in percentage points of 'consequential error' (hottest 30-day mean daily water temperature wrong by more than 0.5 °C), at 70% retention, so negative values favour the screen. Bars are network-clustered 95% intervals; the solid line is zero and the dashed line is the pre-declared threshold Δ = -1.0 percentage point. Archival retrospective panels give -6.35 points [-7.12, -5.52] on development and -7.13 points [-8.97, -5.36] on the reserved holdout. The wave-2 independent confirmation (USGS, Hub'Eau, UK EA; repaired QA) is plotted when its pre-declared success rule is met. The first sealed USGS and multi-agency transport scores remain omitted as withdrawn after the 2026-10-13 audit. Every interval is a 95% network-clustered confidence interval. The right-hand column gives the cases and networks behind each row.

## What is not in the panel

- **real-archive empirical arm** — not plotted. The real archive is the 2422 real incomplete station-years of e2_archive_deployment. Those years carry no truth by construction, so no certificate-minus-length error-rate contrast (or interval) exists for them; the requested row cannot be sourced and is not drawn.
  The closest outcome-agnostic evidence for the real archive is the admission disagreement between the two rules, which needs no truth: on 2422 real incomplete station-years the incumbent unfilled limit admits 83.2% and the peak-window rule 81.2%, and the two disagree on 15.1% of them (`results/v18_closure/e2_archive_deployment/threshold_disagreement.csv`, row 'Johnson unfilled, longest run <= 49 d'). That is a rate of disagreement, not an error-rate contrast, and it has no place on this axis.
