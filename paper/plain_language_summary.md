# Plain Language Summary

Rivers are often monitored every day, but sensors still fail. Recoverability may be predictable before choosing a model. We separate three sources already present in the monitoring record: the repeating seasonal cycle, temperature anomalies shared with surviving stations, and the target station's own short-term memory.

Using only 2006--2015, an equation predicts recovery skill for gaps from 1 to 365 days. It classifies two Upper Jinsha stations as donor-dominated, with nearly flat long-gap predictions, and one as memory-dominated, with skill that decays as gaps lengthen. Fixed artificial outages support this broad shape: correlations with the best-model curves are 0.72--0.95. But the equation is not a ceiling. Stable models exceed it in many gaps, especially at the memory-dominated station.

Neighbouring observations are useful, but lag and permutation tests show correlation rather than identified river transport. B1 depends most on S2 and S2 on B1, so sensor priorities are directional. A second connected river panel is reserved for an evaluate-once test. The equation is a rapid screening tool that requires local calibration; no safe operational fill length is claimed.
