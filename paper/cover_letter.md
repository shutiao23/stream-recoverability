# Cover letter (Water Resources Research)

**Manuscript title:** A Case-Study Covariance Heuristic for Stream-Temperature Recoverability in Two Regulated River Networks

**Dear Editors,**

We submit this Research Article for consideration in *Water Resources Research*. The manuscript connects a familiar hydrological consequence of reservoir releases--altered downstream thermal seasonality and persistence--to a monitoring-network question: which information remains available to reconstruct an outage?

The contribution is a reproducible case-study heuristic, not a new dam classifier, a causal reservoir mechanism, or an imputation-model leaderboard. A train-only covariance calculation separates synchronous donor information from local boundary memory before recovery models are evaluated. In two detailed river networks, the station immediately below a major dam is uniquely memory-dominated from 14- to 90-day horizons. We now treat that association as a descriptive screen. Dam proximity is aliased with network topology, the Jinsha files lack per-value quality assurance, and overlap-aware audits show too few independent clusters for Wilcoxon tests or statistical-frontier confidence intervals.

The paper also makes its generalization boundary central. In an independently frozen panel of 335 United States stream-temperature stations, the frozen primary pooled leave-one-ecoregion-out AUC is 0.407. We retain that number as a preregistered defective diagnostic and report the valid post-hoc level as a mean within-fold AUC of 0.526, still with no national skill. Chattahoochee type labels were frozen before 2023--2025 outcomes; fixed-model scores are labelled post-hoc and are not used as confirmatory headlines.

All main performance results include absolute error. Network-failure effects use leave-one-year-out model selection. The Jinsha change date is reported as method-sensitive, donor falsification restricts interpretation to shared predictive information, and all post-hoc analyses are labelled explicitly.

The public code-only release contains no restricted observational bytes. The Jinsha hydrological and meteorological inputs are third-party records for which redistribution permission was not established. They will be uploaded to GEMS as confidential Data Files for Peer Review, with complete provenance and rights documentation. We request the editor's approval of this restricted-data exception; the manuscript will not be submitted until that approval and a real archival software DOI have been obtained. The current submission gate remains `NO-GO` for those administrative items.

We believe the work fits WRR as an observational hydrology and monitoring-network study that reports where inference is not valid, rather than as a confirmatory mechanism paper.

The authors declare that the manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. Author, funding, conflict-of-interest, and suggested-reviewer information will be supplied in GEMS and must match the final title page.
