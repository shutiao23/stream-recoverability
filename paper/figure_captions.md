# Figure and table captions

## Main figures

**Figure 1. Reservoir-regulation fingerprint across two networks.** (a) Annual minimum stream temperature and (b) annual amplitude at B1, S2, and P3; the dashed line marks first-unit generation at Guanyinyan on 20 December 2014 and shading covers the commissioning transition. (c) Fitting-period temperature range versus lag-30 anomaly autocorrelation for all eight stations. Squares are memory-dominated and circles donor-dominated; colour denotes network. (d) Chattahoochee downstream profile. Temperature range increases and memory decreases away from Buford Dam. Range and autocorrelation are observation-based diagnostics, not a heat-budget attribution.

**Figure 2. Frozen analytic prediction and post-hoc thermal-state control.** Best-model climatology-relative skill and the train-only budget at B1, S2, and P3. Red/black use the original 2006--2015 calibration and denominator; blue re-scores fixed predictions and recalibrates the budget to 2016--2020. The blue analysis overlaps evaluated years and diagnoses nonstationarity; it is not a second confirmatory test. Shading is the 95% anchor/year bootstrap interval for the original best fixed-model envelope.

**Figure 3. Recoverability in relative and absolute units.** Top: climatology-relative skill for five representative stable methods. Values below -0.5 are clipped for readability. Bottom: the same methods' MAE in degrees C, with the paired training-climatology MAE shown as a black dashed line. All points use common artificial cells.

**Figure 4. Best-available node importance.** Mean increase in best-achievable MAE after each singleton station failure, faceted by target station and averaged over the four network gap lengths. The method may reselect after failure, and climatology is a hard error cap. Negative bars are retained finite-sample/model-selection outcomes and do not imply that failure creates physical information.

**Figure 5. Evaluate-once Chattahoochee confirmation.** (a) Solid curves are the observed best-model envelope at 30, 90, and 180 days; dashed curves are the frozen train-only covariance predictions. Red identifies memory-dominated site 02334430 below Buford Dam. (b) Fitting-period anomaly memory versus observed 180-day best skill. The external panel confirms the qualitative donor/memory ordering but not universal prediction magnitudes. Each cell has one frozen external mask and is descriptive rather than a population confidence interval.

## Main tables

**Table 1. Eight-station regulation fingerprint.** Fitting-period observed range, climatological range, anomaly variability and memory, donor and memory budget components, covariance type, within-network memory--range rank, station order, and dam context.

**Table 2. Annual Upper Jinsha thermal statistics.** Minimum, maximum, mean, and amplitude in degrees C for every station and year, 2006--2020.

**Table 3. Frozen and stationarity-controlled budget evaluation.** Prediction correlation, mean absolute skill error, point exceedance count, and lower-confidence-bound exceedance count by station. The 2016--2017 and 2016--2020 rows are post-freeze diagnostics.

**Table 4. Dense recoverability curves in relative and absolute units.** Station, fixed model, gap length, mean skill and 95% interval, model MAE, climatology MAE, and anchor counts.

**Table 5. Corrected best-available node importance.** Full-network and failed-network best MAE, paired climatology cap, most frequently selected methods, impact, and event count by target, gap, and failed station.
