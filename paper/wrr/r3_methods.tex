\section{Data and study design}

\subsection{Comparisons and information available at use}

The design separates three changes that can otherwise produce an apparent screening gain: changing the reconstruction, changing the diagnostic target and adding a representation of the target window. Paired gap experiments hold the reference station-year fixed while varying missing-data geometry. Diagnostic comparisons hold the delivered H30 estimate, learning capacity and calibration resources fixed. Transfer tests then change the station histories while preserving specified model and threshold choices. This sequence distinguishes error propagation, component attribution and deployment performance.

We use three evidence populations. Constructed gaps identify the consequences of timing and duration; a broader mixture of short, long and empirical gaps evaluates admission; recorded-calendar replay examines historically observed missing-data geometries. Complete reference records provide evaluation values in these experiments. Independently backed natural gaps require a separate reference qualification and are not supplied by replay. Original frozen tests and later analyses of opened outcomes retain distinct status throughout.

\subsection{Reference records and independent roles}

The source cohort contains \RSourceStations{} provider-station identities and \RSourceYears{} complete station-years from \RSourceProviders{} providers. Its \RSourceNetworks{} network labels form \RSourceGroups{} connected components when shared target or donor records are accounted for. We use these components as the sampling unit to avoid treating reused records as independent evidence. The primary measurement scope requires provider-documented daily means; uncertain statistics and project-aggregated means are separate sensitivities. Within this scope, the controlled experiment includes \RDocumentedControlledCases{} masks on \RDocumentedYears{} station-years in \RDocumentedGroups{} components. A complete reference has a finite value on every calendar day. Completeness alone establishes neither sensor accuracy nor the absence of provider-estimated values.

New station histories were recruited from the mountain-river resource of \citeA{vanhamel2024,vanhameldata2024}. Because its extreme-value subset cannot define a continuous H30 reference, we reacquired continuous temperatures from Swiss FOEN and French Hub'Eau. We audited all previously used target and donor identities using aliases, coordinates and available catchment connections. Candidate components were randomly assigned development, calibration and test roles before acquisition of daily values. Subsequent completeness and prior-input checks reduced eligibility while retaining those roles.

The resulting cohort contains \RNewStations{} stations: \RNewDevelopment{} for development, \RNewCalibration{} for calibration and \RNewTest{} for testing. The test set includes \RNewSwissTest{} Swiss documented-mean and \RNewFrenchTest{} French project-aggregated records. French daily aggregation requires at least four observations spanning eighteen local-time hours and includes explicitly unqualified source observations. Those records therefore remain outside the primary documented-mean scope. Eligibility requires four complete reference years, leaving at least three permitted complete training years for an evaluated year. Supporting information (SI) documents acquisition and exclusions.

The European source and target records are station-disjoint after audit, although incomplete historical catchment polygons limit the assessment of hydrological connections. A further transfer uses USGS-only source models, removing shared source--target river-flow connections while retaining possible climatic dependence and environmental shift. Descriptive process strata use glacier influence first and a majority-snow threshold of 50\%. Regulation and groundwater classes remain unverified. These descriptive labels do not enter the frozen predictors or thresholds.

\subsection{Gap timing, duration and recorded calendars}

To isolate missing-data geometry, we pair 30-, 60- and 90-day gaps at feasible zero, partial and full overlap with the training-only climatological peak window. Four start realizations are attempted in each station-year cell. Further controls hold the number of missing warm-season days fixed or distribute that budget among contiguous and fragmented gaps. Infeasible combinations remain empty. Across measurement classes, the experiment yields \RControlledCases{} retrospective and \RPriorControlledCases{} earlier-years-only cases. Replicated masks resolve placement effects within records; they do not add independent rivers.

Admission requires a wider population because a long-gap-only design omits records that a simple duration rule could accept. We therefore use lengths of 14, 30, 60, 90, 120, 180 and 270 days, one feasible climatological-overlap class per length, and up to five transplanted empirical geometries. This yields \RAdmissionCases{} retrospective cases, comprising \RStratifiedCases{} single-run and \REmpiricalCases{} empirical geometries, and \RPriorAdmissionCases{} earlier-years-only cases. The mixture defines a screening assay rather than an estimate of natural outage prevalence.

Recorded-calendar replay transfers genuinely incomplete historical shapes onto complete reference years. It does not observe the original incomplete years' unknown values and excludes calendars with no missing or no observed days. Equal-duration event masks fall before, during and after the same longest thermal episode. Project episodes exceed a training-only seasonal 95th percentile for at least three days; published events use a separate two-day definition. Compound heat/low-flow labels use training-month discharge quantiles. Discharge missing on required gap-event days remains unknown.

We cross timing with removal of none, half or all initially usable neighbours, recomputing local reconstruction inputs but leaving admission heads and thresholds unchanged. Fractional removal rounds upward, so a one-neighbour case loses it at both nonzero levels. The new European bundles are single-station: their cofailure contrast would be degenerate. The nondegenerate test uses held-out opened-source multi-station components. Event-dependent masking is informative-missingness stress, not identification of real instrument-failure causes.

\section{Target estimation, screening and validation}

\subsection{The thermal statistic and its reconstruction}

For a complete recorded year $y$,
\begin{equation}
H_{30}(y)=\max_{w\in\mathcal W_{30}}\frac{1}{30}\sum_{t\in w}y_t,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal W_{30}$ contains consecutive windows within that calendar year. Let $M$ denote missing days and $S$ a maximizing true window. A climatological window $S_c$ uses only permitted history. Its overlap $|M\cap S_c|/30$ is available at decision time; true overlap $|M\cap S|/30$ is an evaluation diagnostic, never a screening feature.

The archive-use benchmark asks whether to return an annual magnitude within 0.5\,$^{\circ}$C. Its 5\% selected-risk cap concerns errors exceeding that tolerance under the declared group-sampling scheme. A five-percentage-point retention margin asks for five extra annual magnitudes per hundred original candidates at the common risk criterion. These parameters make the information-return tradeoff explicit; they are experimental benchmarks, not biological thresholds or validated agency service levels.

We fit day-of-year and smooth climatologies, interpolation, neighbour ridge and air-temperature ridge. The common point follows a fixed information priority: usable neighbour prediction, available air prediction, interpolation if the actual longest run is at most 30 days, then smooth and day-of-year climatology. Every hidden day must receive a finite value; observations stay unchanged. This is an information-priority policy, not selection on hidden error. Individual reconstructors and a certificate choosing its own method are separate comparisons.

Writing $\widetilde y$ for a completed year and $\widehat S$ for its maximizing window gives
\begin{align}
H_{30}(\widetilde y)-H_{30}(y)&=B+F,\\
B&=\frac{1}{30}\sum_{t\in S}(\widetilde y_t-y_t),\\
F&=\overline{\widetilde y}_{\widehat S}-\overline{\widetilde y}_{S}\geq0.
\end{align}
$B$ is error carried by the true window, with zero contribution from observed days. $F$ is a \emph{window-switch increment}. Another observed window can replace an underestimated true peak, making $F$ positive while final error stays negative. It is not generally a false-hot-peak channel. A stratified absolute-error association between aligned and misaligned windows is not identified with $F$.

We cross hidden-residual permutation with removal of their hidden mean, including the interaction. Both require truth. A separate deployable correction uses visible flanks and historical residual information with shrinkage. Oracle centering benefits are not operational improvements or evidence of unconditional physical cold bias: conditioning on a true maximum can favor negative error there.

\subsection{Separating the diagnostic target from window representation}

Leave-training-year-out prediction residuals supply contiguous blocks for 128 conditional completed trajectories around the policy reconstruction. Blocks are nominally fourteen days and never bridge missing residual runs. If none exists for a shorter interpolation gap, a complete block as long as its longest run is permitted; long gaps never fall back to independent singletons. Observations stay fixed. Trajectories yield H30 spread/error-scale summaries, probabilistic hidden support, peak-location dispersion, competition and interactions. Bands are raw scenarios, not confidence or risk guarantees.

The uncertain-window score adds window summaries to a generic model containing gap, season, history, donor and reconstructed-H30 disagreement features, plus direct H30 uncertainty. Primary comparisons share quantile boosting, capacity, labels, point and calibration resources. Alternatives include direct H30 uncertainty; actual out-of-fold daily reconstruction scale; complete historical/observed diagnostics; mask-matched historical H30 error; seasonal splines; and small logistic models with and without overlap. Replacing H30 disagreement with daily prediction disagreement tests target awareness, not just explicit overlap. Removing competition, window spread and donor features tests the components.

The original score learns the 0.9 quantile of absolute H30 error. An additional four-cell comparison crosses that objective with direct learning of $P(|H_{30}-\widehat H_{30}|>0.5\mid\mathcal I)$, each using direct H30 diagnostics alone or with added window summaries. All cells share the reconstruction, component roles, boosting depth, iterations, learning rate and leaf size. Complete risk--retention curves, risk at fixed retention and full learning/calibration refits evaluate whether apparent window gains depend on the learning objective. These comparisons use opened data.

A common-point certificate proxy differs from an aligned head for the current policy. A whole-system certificate chooses its own reconstruction and calibrates that point's error; it is not a feature-only comparison. Unsupported scores do not remove candidates. Air2stream-3/8 \cite{piccolroaz2016} and an old-source-trained univariate CSDI reference \cite{tashiro2021} test reconstruction sensitivity on identical masks, with availability and matched-subset policy errors. CSDI has no fully prospective claim. Air2stream forcing calibration and normalization exclude the target year and use earlier years prospectively. Extra meteorology, discharge or neighbours are information increments, not architectural gains.

\subsection{Calibration transport and the independent unit}

Source comparisons use five outer folds with disjoint fitting, calibration and testing components in approximately 60/20/20 proportions, repeated for three seeds. Candidate thresholds are fixed from training scores. The empirical selector uses a calibration component bootstrap and is heuristic. A zero-failure bootstrap can return zero uncertainty without assigning probability to an unseen failure type; more repetitions do not solve that problem.

For a formal alternative, let $A_g$ be within-component mean admission and $B_g$ mean admission times failure. Test $X_g=B_g+0.05(1-A_g)$. A Chernoff--KL upper bound on $E[X_g]$ at most 0.05 implies $E[B_g]/E[A_g]\leq0.05$, with positive denominator. Bonferroni adjustment permits searching training-fixed candidates without monotonic-risk assumptions. This requires independent, deployment-representative components and a predictor independent of calibration. It concerns a group-expectation ratio, not every subgroup, arbitrary archive case risk or unconditional transfer. Even with zero failures and full admission, one threshold needs \RSingleThresholdGroups{} independent groups; 21 candidates need \RGridThresholdGroups{}. Our target calibration cohorts cannot establish this guarantee.

Deployment separates F0, unchanged source model and threshold; F1, the same model with target calibration; and F2, new-development fitting with target calibration. Provider-excluded and USGS-only transfers are further checks. Local historical reconstruction fitting is allowed; hidden target-year labels never select a method. Predictive choices are frozen before target calibration errors; thresholds are locked before test errors.

Earlier-years-only local reconstruction is not a complete forward-in-calendar-time pipeline. Our forward experiment also fits and calibrates the global head through 2018, evaluates 2019--2022, and computes local climatology, donor relationships and residual diagnostics from earlier calendar years. This is a retrospective forward hindcast using current-vintage observations and reanalysis, not a real-time-vintage or prospective field trial; historical publication and revision vintages are unavailable. Its smaller pool is separate. Whole-procedure uncertainty refits learning and calibration in \RWholeRetRefits{} component resamples per protocol, unlike intervals conditional on fitted procedures. Learning curves use repeated subsets. Power projections retain training/calibration variability and do not establish common-risk feasibility under shift.

\subsection{Numerical delivery, hot-year decisions and unknown records}

The primary label asks whether H30 exceeds the 90th percentile of permitted training-year H30. This finite-history local anomaly proxy is not a verified ecological or regulatory standard. Fixed 18 and 20\,$^{\circ}$C labels are sensitivities. False-hot, missed-hot, correct and unknown outcomes share the original pool, with separate hot/cool refusal, threshold proximity, coverage and regional-frequency distortion.

With false-hot cost one, loss is $(c_{FN}FN+FP+c_AU)/N$. Declared missed-hot costs are 1, 5 and 10; unknown-answer costs are 0, 0.01, 0.025, 0.05, 0.1 and 0.25. Refused records remain unknown or interval-valued, not automatically repaired. These are not measured agency preferences; intervals across the grid are descriptive, not simultaneous guarantees.

A use-matched rule estimates $p=P(H_{30}>\tau\mid\mathcal I)$ and minimizes the conditional losses $c_{FN}p$, $1-p$ and $c_A$ for cool, hot and unknown answers. Probability fitting and Platt calibration use disjoint groups. Constant hot, cool and unknown answers and random rejection at matched retention provide further deployable references. At retention $a$, expected random-rejection loss is $aL_{\rm all}+(1-a)c_A$; its counts are expectations. These opened-data comparisons test whether magnitude screening also serves the classification loss.

A refused magnitude can retain an identified hot label. Let $L$ be the largest mean of a \emph{fully observed} 30-day window, or minus infinity if none exists. Since $L\leq H_{30}(y)$, $L$ above threshold establishes hot conditions against the complete recorded reference. An 80\%-observed mean is not a bound. This secondary rescue leaves magnitude admission unchanged. Observation margins of 0.1 and 0.2\,$^{\circ}$C are scenarios, not certified sensor bounds; $L$ below threshold never establishes cool conditions.

\subsection{Failure diagnosis and robustness}

Information-loss experiments remove donors on identical masks and recompute reconstruction and uncertainty. Fitting and calibration information are crossed; this does not identify all causes of international shift. Every accepted Swiss failure is traced by station, year, gap and error sign. Flow and hidden-day proxy residuals explain evaluation outcomes only and do not enter the original frozen screen. Common-population contrasts and truth-assisted nested gaps hold the true 30-day window hidden while extending left, right or symmetrically, retaining boundary distances and infeasibility.

Further assays examine whether visible season and hydrological state can describe slowly varying bias and short residual dependence. They compare a slow-plus-short conditional residual model, 3/5/10-year histories on identical feasible target years, boundary and fragmented gaps, and complete reconstruction--admission--decision systems. Each reconstruction learns and calibrates its own error event; flow-enhanced inputs form a separate information tier. Regional forward-event holdouts remove the target provider's connected components, fit and calibrate global scores through 2014, and evaluate 2015/2018/2022 using strictly earlier local histories. Provider remains a regional proxy. Joint learning/calibration/evaluation budget experiments combine bounded paired-gain evidence \cite{hoeffding1963} with risk evidence; their design, the development assays and a separate future-study specification are reported in SI.
