\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Thermal-statistic fitness requires a target-specific diagnosis}

The central finding is that the target of diagnosis matters more consistently than the addition of an explicit peak-window representation. H30 diagnostics retain more usable estimates than daily diagnostics under matched comparisons, whereas added window summaries do not establish the predefined practical increment over strong direct references. This extends earlier evidence on missing-data timing and seasonal thermal-peak reconstruction \cite{johnson2021,beaufort2022} by separating the diagnostic target from its representation. Seasonal support is already known to matter; the additional contribution is the comparison of information needed to assess the derived statistic.

The contrast has a statistical explanation. Daily errors summarize the series, while H30 accumulates errors over a selected warm period and then maximizes over possible periods. A sustained offset can therefore matter more than dispersed daily deviations with a similar aggregate error. Once a complete conditional H30 distribution is available for a fixed delivered point, it determines the probability of exceeding tolerance. Window summaries may improve estimation of that distribution, particularly when the distribution model misses relevant state or dependence. Their benefit must be demonstrated against the direct target-risk reference.

The error decomposition also qualifies a common interpretation of peak reconstruction. A positive switch increment can compensate cold error within the true peak, so its sign alone does not identify a falsely hot reconstructed extreme. The strong oracle response to residual centering indicates error structure that affects the statistic, but the small visible-information correction shows that this structure is not yet equally recoverable in practice. The intervention interaction and conditioning on a true maximum further limit physical attribution from the decomposition.

\subsection{Calibration transport depends on the deployment information}

The new-station experiments distinguish informative ranking from transferable calibration. A score can order source cases usefully while its threshold admits too many target failures. In the present transfer, station conditions and reconstruction inputs change together: source reconstructions often use neighbors, whereas Swiss reconstructions rely mainly on air temperature. Source donor removal isolates part of that information change, but does not reproduce the full target error distribution. Consequently, the international contrast cannot be assigned to a single hydrological cause.

The concentrated cold failures at CH\_2327 are consistent with a peak-season air-proxy mismatch and motivate tests of conditional bias and multiweek persistence. They do not establish whether low flow, melt inputs or another process produced that mismatch. This distinction complements evidence that similar in-distribution river-temperature accuracy need not imply similar behavior under environmental shift \cite{topp2023}. Predictive generalization and transport of a calibrated admission threshold require related but separate tests.

Local calibration improves the observed operating point, but its interpretation depends on the number and composition of independent examples. Record-overlap components prevent duplication without ensuring independent catchment climates or heat events. The formal rule requires deployment-representative components and controls a group-expectation ratio. Empty formal admission indicates insufficient evidence for that rule, rather than universal record unusability. The calibration-subset results illustrate why additional masks cannot substitute for additional independent calibration units.

\subsection{Archive value depends on the requested output}

An archive user may require an H30 magnitude, a hot-year label or a regional frequency. Each output has a different relation to reconstruction error and refusal. A complete observed window can establish a hot label after magnitude refusal, although few labels were recovered here. A below-threshold observed window cannot establish a cool year. At regional scale, selective accuracy must be considered together with unknown fractions, hot/cool refusal and original-pool frequency bounds.

The different policy rankings follow from these different objectives. A numerical tolerance constrains magnitude error, whereas hot-year loss depends on threshold crossing and the relative costs of missed, false and unknown answers. The experimental cost grid exposes that distinction without estimating ecological damage, field-revisit expense or agency preferences. Similarly, the finite-history anomaly threshold describes local thermal conditions rather than a biological standard.

A reduction relative to reconstructing all can coexist with higher loss than a constant unknown answer or another directly optimized policy. The stronger references therefore change the interpretation of the original H3 comparison. An application that requires a minimum information return must specify that requirement before evaluation. Likewise, adaptive archive retrieval is meaningful only when an actual record, feasible retrieval action and measured cost exist. Freely available backup and forcing data should be incorporated into the information set.

\subsection{Evidence boundaries and the next independent comparison}

The experiments require complete reference and training years, limiting their representation of intermittent and persistently gapped archives. The new station histories are European mountain catchments with few independent calibration/test components and no within-bundle donor variation. Available glacier and snow attributes do not supply verified regulation or groundwater classes. Measurement definitions, quality flags and project aggregation further constrain comparisons. Dry channels require particular care because absent water can change the definition of a temperature estimand.

Constructed gaps identify error responses to prescribed missingness, while calendar replay conditions on recorded gap shapes. Neither supplies the unknown temperatures of the original naturally incomplete years or identifies the causes of instrument failure. Derived and published thermal-event definitions also differ, and incomplete discharge leaves some compound conditions unknown. Current-vintage forward hindcasts restrict information to earlier calendar years without recovering the data vintages that would have been available in real time.

The developed probability comparison strengthens the direct references while retaining a finite model budget. Its neural window head improves retention relative to the matched boosted direct head, but the native CDF rule already has higher retention and lower sample risk. This positive within-head increment remains relevant; it establishes neither a win over the strongest direct reference nor a universal negative conclusion about window information. Undercoverage and conditional cold bias likewise constrain interpretation of the scenario distributions. The source-law joint-budget experiment supports no 80\% design in its evaluated grid and supplies no recruitment target for a new neural candidate or climate (SI).

Independent-backup qualification remains a substantive evidence gap. No DFO pair-year meets the required complete-history support \cite{dfo2024}. The two separately evaluated short-history records contain cold-season partial daily deficits and retain their observed hot windows, making zero H30 error weak evidence for difficult recovery. A further metadata-led search acquired paired channels at seven USGS stream sites, but none of 25 candidate pair-years passed all reference requirements (SI). These exclusions preserve the original support and sensor-consistency criteria.

The present evidence therefore supports a study of target diagnosis and failure boundaries. A new representation requires a separate independent comparison in which predictive choices, calibration resources, deployment units and intended use are fixed before outcomes. Joint success must combine risk evidence with a practical gain over both native-CDF and matched-capacity direct-risk references. Additional masks, a favorable subgroup or an oracle intervention cannot replace that comparison.

\section{Conclusions}

Using \RSourceYears{} complete reference station-years and paired gap experiments, we show that thermal-statistic fitness depends on both missing-data geometry and the target of diagnosis. Equal 30-day gaps differ in climatology-based H30 error by \RPosition{}\,$^{\circ}$C when moved into the climatological peak. H30 diagnostics increase locally recalibrated Swiss retention by \RTestRetTargetDailyGain{} points relative to daily diagnostics. Additional peak-window summaries do not establish the predefined five-point advantage over strong direct target-risk references.

The original source-calibrated rule incurs \RTestRetFrozenRisk{}\% failures against a 5\% target on \RTestRetCases{} Swiss cases; local recalibration lowers observed risk to \RTestRetRecalibratedRisk{}\%, with population reliability unresolved by the small independent cohort. Classification loss can favor a different action from numerical screening. Assessing an incomplete thermal archive consequently requires separate evidence for the reconstructed statistic, its calibrated admission rule and the requested output.
