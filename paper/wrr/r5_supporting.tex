The developed neural reference conditions on water and available air, unlike the original fixed univariate reference. Both new widths share data and development limits. It is therefore an information and budget change relative to the original reference, not proof of an architectural improvement. The developed system supplies its entire empirical H30 law to every point arm, including native tolerance-exceedance probability; extra window features cannot be credited merely for supplying previously omitted direct uncertainty. Group-disjoint development/calibration/testing roles and unavailable original candidates remain explicit. Thirty-two scenarios per case are evaluated using twelve parallel case-chunk workers per role, one torch thread and batch thirty-two each. An optional shared tail scheduler adds thirty-two workers calling exactly the same sampler after byte-agreement checks on reconstructed inputs. Each chunk has a distinct deterministic seed. Repeated-input batching changes random-number ordering but not the conditional sampling law; scheduling cannot change cached chunk outputs. This remains a finite development budget, not a probability-model ceiling.

The Canadian archive is staged with metadata and source-model locks before thermal outcomes. Sensor agreement and minimum daily coverage are fixed before reconstruction errors. One dependent provincial cohort is retained rather than assigning independent certainty to sensors or repeated references. These are conditional support sensitivities, not validated physical or sensor-error bounds; they explain why this sample is weak evidence for peak recovery.
