\section{Risk estimand and proof}
For a fixed predictor and candidate threshold, let each deployment component contribute within-component mean admission $A_g$ and failure-and-admission $B_g$. Since $0\leq B_g\leq A_g\leq1$,
\[
 X_g=B_g+r(1-A_g)\in[0,1],\qquad r=0.05.
\]
The desired ratio is $E[B_g]/E[A_g]$, not mean component-conditional risk and not arbitrary case-weighted risk. For $G$ independent deployment-representative components, invert the bounded-variable Chernoff--KL inequality to obtain an upper mean bound $U$ satisfying
\[
 G\,\mathrm{kl}(\bar X,U)=\log(m/\alpha),
\]
where $m$ is the number of training-fixed candidate thresholds. A union bound covers candidate search, including nonmonotone risk. If any threshold has $U\leq r$ and positive admission, then $E[B_g]\leq r E[A_g]$ on the simultaneous-validity event. No result follows under arbitrary target shift or dependent components. The zero-mean upper bound is $1-(\alpha/m)^{1/G}$, strictly positive.

Even the ideal all-admit, zero-failure observation therefore needs \RSingleThresholdGroups{} groups for one candidate or \RGridThresholdGroups{} for twenty-one. Target calibration is too small. Empty certified admission is insufficient evidence, not observed risk zero or a recommendation to discard every record.

The empirical bootstrap rule is a separate descriptive operating policy. Constructed populations include twelve, forty and one hundred perfectly dependent-within-group cases and a rare high-risk group, with uninformative scores. These are counterexamples to general calibration claims, not measured frequencies in rivers. Test intervals describe variation over components conditional on fixed predictors; they cannot supply probability for an unobserved failure type.

\section{Mechanism, trajectories and the next independent study}
The exact-hidden 30/90-day length subset has 396 distinct station-years represented by 400 network-target-year rows. The original documented-mean mechanism pool has 4,720 distinct station-years represented by 4,884 such rows. Shared rows remain clustered in their complete record components. Common-subset contrasts accompany the original population. In the added nested assay, the true maximizing thirty-day interval defines a hidden seed, extended left, right or symmetrically to sixty and ninety days. A thirty-day nonoverlapping control is displaced ninety days in the first feasible predetermined direction. All method contrasts use the same feasible years within direction, retaining boundary distances. These true-window designs identify sensitivity in an artificial mechanism assay and cannot be used as deployment rules.

The experimental state trajectory model separates a sixty-day smoothed historical residual path from shorter residuals. Historical-year analogs are weighted by visible water residuals and available air/flow anomalies relative to permitted monthly training history; a ten-percent uniform mixture preserves some analog diversity. Fourteen-day short blocks are matched within thirty seasonal days and never cross years. Historical residuals exclude the target year and all future years prospectively. Observations remain fixed. Two usable residual years are required, with unsupported cases explicit. Original raw blocks, visible correction and this candidate are compared on their common support, including CRPS, band width/coverage, hot-probability Brier score and warm/low-flow conditions. Nested samples of 32, 128 and 512 quantify Monte Carlo sensitivity. More draws cannot correct an incorrect residual distribution.

The reconstruction factorial refits each method's own numerical-error event on development groups and calibrates on the separate calibration role before evaluating the original test role.
