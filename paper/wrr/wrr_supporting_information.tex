\section*{Introduction}

This supporting information records what belongs to the record rather than to
the argument: data provenance, protocol history, the predictions declared for
this round that were not met, the runs that were discarded and why, and the
reproduction path. Nothing here is required to follow the article.

\section{Data sources}

Daily water-temperature series were assembled from nine public providers
(Table~\ref{tab:si-providers}); two further agencies (SMHI, UK Environment
Agency) supply the never-developed transport panel. Each provider's product,
documented statistic, quality codes, licence and acquisition route were
re-verified against the provider's own documentation on 2026-10-12
(\texttt{results/wrr\_submission\_ready/measurement\_provenance\_dictionary.csv}).
Eight of the eleven publish under a named open licence and none required a
data-use agreement, but LUBW reserves copyright on its raw values: those seven
networks are not redistributed here and are read locally through LUBW's own
service.

Station coordinates were assembled for all 457 stations: from the providers'
own catalogues already held in the repository for FOEN, CHMI, SYKE, LUBW, GKD

the repository records its source.

Daily 2\,m air temperature is NASA POWER (MERRA-2), 1 January 1981 to 31 August
2026, one point per station at the product's native resolution: 16{,}679 days
for each of the 457 stations. An earlier version of this study used a much
smaller forcing table covering 2.3\% of development cases; both are retained and
Section 4.4.2 of the article compares them.

Basin characteristics for the United States subset are GAGES-II, joined on the
eight-digit gauge identifier and matching 140 of 204 eligible stations.

\section{Quality control and the definition of a network}

A \emph{network} is a provider's group of stations on a common river system,
which is the unit at which donor stations are available to one another. Networks
are the clustering unit for every interval in the article, because station-years
within a network share weather, regulation and instrumentation.

Waterbody class was assigned per network from the provider's own naming and an
explicit map; seas, lakes, canals, estuaries and deltas are listed and excluded
rather than averaged into a river result. Reference series are never imputed: a
calendar year with any missing day contributes no truth. Stations with fewer
than four fully observed calendar years are excluded, because the
leave-one-year-out estimator protocol requires them. The resulting denominator,
with every exclusion and its count, is in the repository. The same stations
contribute 2422 station-years with at least 360 daily slots and at least one
missing day; those form the deployment panel of Section 4.4.3 of the article.

Three measurement strata are reported rather than merged. In the study panel,
five providers document a daily mean that the parser reads directly (USGS, FOEN,
GKD Bayern, NVE, LUBW); two are project-derived daily means of the provider's own
sub-daily values (CHMI's $T_O$ instantaneous series, Rijkswaterstaat's raw
\texttt{NVT} readings); and two are not a documented daily mean at all (SYKE's
08:00 nearshore reference reading, ARSO's undocumented statistic). The transport
panel adds the UK Environment Agency, which documents a daily mean, and SMHI and
Hub'Eau, whose daily statistics are undocumented and continuous respectively;
those sit in the assurance strata recorded by the transport seal. Restricting the development panel to the documented-daily-mean
stratum gives a contrast of $-5.7$ points ($[-6.5,-4.9]$, 84 networks) against
$-6.4$ for the full panel, and the reserved panel $-6.6$ ($[-7.9,-5.3]$, 29
networks) against $-7.1$; the sealed confirmation is USGS-only and lies entirely
inside the documented stratum
(\texttt{amendments/provider\_contract\_sensitivity/}). Each provider's
product, documented statistic, quality codes, licence and acquisition route are
tabulated with verbatim evidence in
\texttt{measurement\_provenance\_dictionary.csv} and
\texttt{current\_data\_acquisition.csv}.

Transport-panel panels receive one additional structural quality-control pass
before sealing, applied identically to every provider and recorded before any
score: warm-season (May--September) runs of three or more consecutive days at
exactly $0.0\,^{\circ}$C are blanked as stuck-sensor values. Two such runs were
found in the UK Environment Agency ingest, one of them inside a hottest-30
window; the rule and its counts are in
\texttt{multi\_agency\_ingest/QC\_SUMMARY.json} and the per-provider copies in
\texttt{multi\_agency\_ingest/uk\_ea/qc\_report.csv} and
\texttt{multi\_agency\_ingest/smhi/qc\_report.csv}.

\section{Protocol history}

The study was pre-registered before the reserved networks were opened on
2026-09-23. Three aspects of that freeze were incomplete or wrong and are
recorded here rather than silently repaired.

\textbf{The incumbent's imputed limit was mis-stated.} An earlier version placed
the comparison at a thirteen-week (91-day) limit attributed to Johnson et al.
(2021). The source says twenty weeks. The analysis code now uses 140 days and
every artifact that used the old value was re-run; Section 4.4.1 of the article
reports the corrected operating point. The direction of the error flattered the
incumbent at that point, so correcting it reduces the screen's advantage there
from 6.2 to 4.4 percentage points.

\textbf{The weighting between outage arms was not declared.} The case set mixes
an empirical arm, drawn from the outage geometry the same networks experience,
with a stratified arm built to span the decision space. Their base rates differ
by an order of magnitude. The pre-registered test was run on the pooled set, as
declared; Section 4.2.3 reports the decomposition and states that the declared
effect is not met on the empirical arm alone.

\textbf{The natural scale of the peak has two values.} Computed over the full
eligible roster it is 1.06\,$^{\circ}$C; recomputed inside the development case
table it is 1.08\,$^{\circ}$C. The primary tolerance is 0.50\,$^{\circ}$C
throughout except in cross-metric comparisons, where each endpoint's scale is
recomputed on the same footing. The tolerance sweep shows the axis ordering is
unchanged between 0.25 and 1.0 standard deviations.

\section{Confirmation, transport and the amendment register}

\textbf{The sealed confirmation.} Thirty-nine USGS HUC4 networks outside the
v17/v18 development panel (91 stations, 18 HUC2 contexts, 8973 designed outage
cases) were ingested, hashed and scored once with the frozen certificate at the
pre-registered operating point. The pooled contrast is $-7.9$ points
($[-10.2,-5.8]$) and the empirical arm $-1.7$ ($[-3.0,-0.7]$). The panel is
outside the development programme but is not untouched by the wider programme: a
repo-wide, provider-aware audit of every artifact carrying a station identifier
finds 24 of the 39 networks in earlier programme iterations, so the earlier
``never used'' label was wrong and is corrected in
\texttt{AMENDMENT\_prior\_access\_sealed\_panel.md}. The result does not rest
on the touched networks: on the 15 networks with no outcome-bearing prior use the
pooled contrast is $-9.4$ points ($[-12.4,-6.0]$) with an empirical-arm gain of
$-3.7$ ($[-5.8,-1.6]$), and on the 45 outcome-clean stations it is $-8.3$
($[-10.4,-6.4]$). Those subsets are read from the already-sealed score and are a
transparency diagnostic, not new confirmation.

\textbf{The transport test.} The confirmation is single-agency, so the
pre-registered transport protocol
(\texttt{MULTI\_AGENCY\_TRANSPORT\_PROTOCOL.md}) recruits agencies absent from
the v17 roster and scores the same frozen configuration once on them. Three
amendments were recorded before sealing: prior access is judged per station
against every artifact in the repository (metadata catalogues keyed by
\texttt{site\_id} carry no series values and are not series use), providers
whose published values are labelled uncontrolled form a separate assurance
stratum reported alongside the pooled verdict, and networks from a provider that
took part in development may enter only as a labelled secondary stratum. The
protocol fixes the primary endpoint, tolerance, retention, the pooled decision
rule ($\Delta \le -1.0$ percentage points with a clustered interval excluding
zero and at least two providers in the same direction) and the requirement that
the pooled and quality-checked-only verdicts agree. It also discloses that it was
designed after the USGS score was known, so it is a transport test and never a
pristine first confirmation.

The sealed transport panel holds 53 networks: 32 primary networks from Sweden
(SMHI, 16), England (UK Environment Agency, 2) and France (Hub'Eau, 14) across
three climate contexts and four assurance strata combinations, plus 21 Norwegian
(NVE) basins as the secondary stratum. All 291 candidate stations are unused
anywhere in the repository, the 47 Hub'Eau and 143 SMHI stations sit in the
provider-published-unqualified stratum, and one structural quality-control pass
(June--September runs of three or more days at exactly $0.0\,^{\circ}$C blanked
as stuck sensors) was applied identically to every provider before the seal. The
primary panel is 17\,826 cases: pooled contrast $-4.6$ points ($[-6.5,-3.3]$),
with SMHI $-4.3$, Hub'Eau $-4.9$ and the UK EA $-6.4$ (2 networks, wide
interval); the quality-checked stratum alone is $-6.2$ ($[-8.4,-4.5]$) and the
empirical arm $-1.9$ ($[-3.3,-1.0]$). The pre-declared rule is met on both the
pooled primary panel and the quality-checked stratum, and the two agree. The
secondary NVE stratum is $-6.1$ ($[-8.7,-4.6]$) and the extended pooled estimate
$-5.2$ ($[-6.7,-4.1]$).

\textbf{The amendment register.} Each adversarial finding closed after the
freeze has an amendment directory holding the recomputation, the preserved
pre-fix bytes and a report: instrument error under a locked method
(\texttt{step22\_noise\_post\_fix}), support-width uncertainty and the
fixed-centre dilation test (\texttt{e6\_support\_width\_uncertainty}),
exact-donor-count mechanism control (\texttt{step5\_mechanism\_equal\_k}), the
climatological-peak proxy's misclassification rate
(\texttt{peak\_proxy\_bridge}), provider-contract sensitivity
(\texttt{provider\_contract\_sensitivity}), a faithful reimplementation of the
cited air-regression baseline (\texttt{faithful\_baseline}), hidden-bias
sensitivity (\texttt{mnar\_pattern\_mixture}), cold-season support axes
(\texttt{winter\_regulatory}), and the sealed panel's prior-access diagnostic
(\texttt{sealed\_usgs\_prior\_access\_diagnostic}). The maintenance
counterfactual was recomputed under multi-year outage placement
(\texttt{e1\_maintenance\_recompute}) and remains exploratory.

\section{Predictions that failed}

Three predictions were written down before the analyses that tested them and
were not met. All three are in the article; they are gathered here because a
reader looking for them should not have to hunt.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{The support-restricted advantage will decay monotonically in the
    support width.} It does not: it is a hump peaking at a 60-day support.
  \item \emph{The estimand's own support is the best conditioning set at every
    support width.} It is not: below 30 days a fixed 30-day window beats it.
  \item \emph{For the sine-fit phase, two hand-chosen transition windows will
    beat both peak-window overlap and outage length.} They beat peak overlap
    decisively and tie outage length.
\end{enumerate}

Failures 1 and 2 have one cause, which we then measured: the support's location
is itself uncertain, so the conditioning set has to be the support dilated by
that uncertainty. Failure 3 has a related cause: a hand-drawn window is a crude
approximation to an influence function that is spread over half the year.

Three further numbers changed under a corrected comparison rather than under new
data, and the corrected values are the ones reported. The instrument-error check
had evaluated its two arms with two different reconstruction choices; with one
method on both arms the perturbation moves the reserved contrast from 6.21 to
6.22 points instead of from 7.06 to 6.67, and the apparent reduction disappears.
The donor-count control had not actually held the donor count fixed (the paired
cases differ by up to seven donors in a third of cases); an exact-match
recomputation gives $+0.084\,^{\circ}$C (0.064 to 0.110), so the claim survives
and the interval is restated. The support-width crossover is an interval, not a
day: the bootstrap gives 77 to 107 days and the curve is a hump peaking near 60
days, not a monotone decay.

A fourth statement was \emph{withdrawn} rather than falsified. An earlier version
framed the standing guidance as getting an ordering backwards. On the guidance's
own estimands it does not; Section 4.2.2 of the article reports that a completeness
threshold is the correct axis for the sine-fit mean and within 0.02 AUC of the
best for the phase.

\section{Runs that were discarded}

Two defects made specific artifacts unusable and both were repaired before the
reserved panel was opened: outage realizations were seeded from a process-salted
hash, so the case table could not be regenerated, and worker results were
concatenated in completion order, so the row order was not reproducible even
though the content was. A third defect affected the calibration rather than the
data: an additive, per-estimator conformal scheme under-covered, because
selecting the smallest of several bounds is a winner's curse that per-estimator
calibration cannot see. In all three cases the affected numbers were discarded
rather than reported alongside the repaired ones, and the pipeline was re-run in
full.

\section{Verification and reproduction}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The case table is byte-reproducible including row order, pinned by a
    test.
  \item The extended endpoint table regenerates the same 51{,}524 realizations
    from the same deterministic seeds, which is how its rows line up with the
    main case table; a test checks the alignment.
  \item No feature may read a day the outage hides; enforced in the feature
    constructor and pinned by a test.
  \item Every table cell and audited inline number in the article is re-derived
    from the artifacts by an audit script that the test suite runs.
  \item The schematic figures are generated from editable draw.io sources held
    in the repository; a test checks that each exported PDF is newer than its
    source.
  \item The claim registry binds every number allowed into the abstract, key
    points and conclusions to an artifact and is checked by
    \texttt{scripts/254\_wrr\_claim\_binding\_check.py}, which recomputes the
    allowed numbers and flags any barred number that appears in those regions.
  \item Every input the current study reads is listed with a byte count and a
    SHA-256 in \texttt{current\_data\_manifest.csv} (146 networks, 0 missing),
    and every ingest for the transport panel records its own hashes before the
    seal is written.
  \item Failed worker runs cannot leave a success table behind: the mechanism
    recomputation writes its summary last inside a hash-bound generation
    directory and re-verifies the generation on read.
\end{itemize}
