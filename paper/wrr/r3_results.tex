\section{Results}

\subsection{Equal coverage losses have different thermal consequences}

Gap position changes H30 error even when missing duration is held fixed. Moving a 30-day gap from zero to full climatological-peak overlap raises climatology-based absolute error by \RPosition{}\,$^{\circ}$C [\RPositionLow{}, \RPositionHigh{}] in documented daily means (Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}a). Broader provider coverage and controls for missing warm-season days retain this position effect. Its magnitude varies with reconstruction and auxiliary information (SI Tables S4--S6).

Length retains an effect. Where the true peak is entirely hidden, extending 30 to 90 days raises climatology error by \RLength{}\,$^{\circ}$C [\RLengthLow{}, \RLengthHigh{}], and interpolation error by \RInterpLength{}\,$^{\circ}$C [\RInterpLengthLow{}, \RInterpLengthHigh{}]. This exact-hidden length contrast uses 396 distinct station-years in 98 components, versus 4,720 station-years for the position contrast. Their magnitudes cannot rank position against length across different populations. Supplementary common-population and nested-gap results retain extension direction and boundary distance (SI). Fragmentation changes true support exposure despite equal total warm missing days. Target-aware assessment is warranted; one overlap number cannot replace completeness information.

\subsection{Window switching often offsets cold peak-window errors}

H30 responds to both error within the true peak and the opportunity for another window to replace it. The decomposition holds to numerical precision, with a nonnegative switch increment in every finite case (SI Table S7). Yet \RCompensatingSwitchShare{}\% of positive climatology increments still end below the reference H30. Switching therefore often compensates underestimation of the true peak (Figure~\ref{fig:channels}a).

Climatology oracle mean absolute errors are \ROracleRaw{}\,$^{\circ}$C originally, \ROraclePermuted{} after permutation, \ROracleDemeaned{} after centering and \ROracleBoth{} after both. The interaction is \ROracleInteraction{}\,$^{\circ}$C, so improvement percentages are not additive mechanism shares. The visible-information correction improves policy MAE by only \RDeployableImprovement{}\,$^{\circ}$C [\RDeployableLow{}, \RDeployableHigh{}]. The oracle diagnoses structure without demonstrating a large available correction.

\subsection{Target diagnostics add information beyond daily accuracy}
\label{sec:diagnostic_results}

Diagnosing the intended statistic improves screening at matched reconstruction and learning capacity. Replacing daily disagreement with H30 disagreement increases source retention by \RTargetDailyMin{}--\RTargetDailyMax{} percentage points. On independent Swiss admission records after local recalibration, the increase is \RTestRetTargetDailyGain{} points [\RTestRetTargetDailyLow{}, \RTestRetTargetDailyHigh{}]. This positive result concerns the diagnostic target.

Additional window summaries provide a smaller and less stable increment. On the admission mixture, uncertain-window source retention exceeds the matched H30-generic score by \RRetGainMin{}--\RRetGainMax{} points across seeds, below the predefined five-point practical margin (Figure~\ref{fig:information}). Earlier-years-only local inputs give \RPriorLocalGainMin{}--\RPriorLocalGainMax{} points, but do not constitute a global forward pipeline. Full-procedure intervals remain broad: \RWholeRetGain{} points [\RWholeRetLow{}, \RWholeRetHigh{}] retrospectively and \RWholePriorGain{} [\RWholePriorLow{}, \RWholePriorHigh{}] in the calendar-time forward experiment.

Direct H30 uncertainty gives essentially the same conditional source retention as the larger window representation. Their conditional paired intervals lie within the predefined two-point equivalence band, whereas whole-procedure intervals do not establish equivalence. Complete historical diagnostics are also competitive. This distinguishes target-specific diagnostics from a necessary uncertain-window representation, not models with and without all peak information. Small-model support features, flexible seasonal alternatives, daily-versus-H30 disagreement and component removals appear together (SI Tables S9--S13), rather than selecting a favorable ablation.

The value of explicit overlap also depends on the comparator. Its increment is \RSmallSupportMin{}--\RSmallSupportMax{} points for a small logistic model but \RSplineSupportMin{} to \RSplineSupportMax{} points for a flexible seasonal spline. H1 therefore supports target-specific diagnosis without establishing that one explicit support variable is necessary.

\subsection{Calibration shift concentrates failures across river conditions}

The source-calibrated threshold does not retain its empirical risk target after unchanged transfer. On \RTestRetCases{} Swiss documented-mean admission cases from \RNewSwissTest{} held-out components, F0 retains \RTestRetFrozenRetention{}\% and incurs \RTestRetFrozenRisk{}\% failures, against the 5\% calibration target (Figure~\ref{fig:transfer}). F1 local recalibration changes these values to \RTestRetRecalibratedRetention{}\% and \RTestRetRecalibratedRisk{}\%; F2 local fitting and calibration give \RTestRetLocalRetention{}\% and \RTestRetLocalRisk{}\%. The seven-component intervals establish neither population risk above 5\% for F0 nor a population guarantee below 5\% for F1 or F2.

\subsection{Numerical screening and classification have different benefits}

Returning more sufficiently accurate H30 magnitudes does not necessarily minimize hot-year classification loss. In the Swiss admission cohort, F0 refuses \RTestRetHotRefusal{}\% of true hot cases, \RTestRetCoolRefusal{}\% of cool cases and \RTestRetOverallRefusal{}\% overall. Refusal changes the evaluated population as well as the number of available labels (Figure~\ref{fig:utility}; SI Table S24).

At missed-hot cost five and unknown-answer cost 0.05 relative to a false-hot answer, loss changes by \RTestRetMagnitudeLossDifference{} per original candidate [\RTestRetMagnitudeLossLow{}, \RTestRetMagnitudeLossHigh{}] versus reconstructing all. This is not sufficient to justify the selective policy: its absolute loss is \RTestRetMagnitudeAbsoluteLoss{}, whereas declaring every record unknown costs only 0.05. We added this deterministic comparator in a post-test audit without changing predictions or the predefined cost grid (SI Table S23). Some other declared cells beat both fixed alternatives at their point estimates, not under a simultaneous or measured management-utility guarantee. With inexpensive errors and costly abstention, even the reconstruct-all comparison can reverse. Observed-hot rescue adds only \RTestRetJudgementRescued{} labels here and \RTestPriorJudgementRescued{} prospectively. H3 holds only for its specified reconstruct-all scenario comparison, not a generally superior operational policy.
