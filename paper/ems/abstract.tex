Long-term analyses of river temperature commonly discard incomplete years, but how this screening affects annual heat extremes and their trends has rarely been quantified against reconstruction. We present an open, leakage-safe benchmarking framework that jointly masks multi-year station archives, reconstructs annual maxima of 30-day mean water temperature (H30), and scores the resulting trends against complete-record references at 41 European and US stations with a median of 14 reference years. Fully observed 30-day windows in incomplete years exceeded complete-year records at 20 of 96 long-record stations. Under realistic gap calendars, warm-season screening and full reconstruction gave trend RMSEs of 0.114 and 0.067 $^{\circ}$C per decade, both below the median trend standard error of 0.59 $^{\circ}$C per decade. Under severe 30--90-day summer gaps, reconstruction reduced RMSE from 0.446 to 0.170 $^{\circ}$C per decade. Interval undercoverage concentrated in air-temperature-based reconstructions in Bavaria, and no method won universally: calendar-aware year selection helped at fixed year counts but less than reconstruction. The framework, implemented as open-source gapbench 1.2.0, separates year-selection, reconstruction and Monte Carlo errors; code is available in a public repository, with an archival DOI pending.
