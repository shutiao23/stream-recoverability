Cover letter

Environmental Modelling & Software

Manuscript title: Discard or reconstruct? A leakage-safe benchmark of gap handling for annual heat extremes and their trends in river-temperature archives

Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript above for consideration as a research article in Environmental Modelling & Software. The work combines a reusable evaluation method, an open-source software implementation, and an applied case study, which is why we believe it fits the journal's scope.

The methodological contribution is a leakage-safe, joint whole-archive benchmark. Multiyear station records are masked together; hidden values never enter the local fit or the year-selection rule; and screening and reconstruction are scored on the same reference-year set. The design separates year-set error, reconstruction error, and their cross term, so the effect of discarding incomplete years can be compared with the effect of reconstructing them on a common scale.

The applied case study evaluates 41 European and US stations in 17 connected groups, with 805 controlled-gap archives and 818 empirical gap calendars. Under controlled 30-90-day gaps, screening incomplete years inflated total trend uncertainty by 21.5 percent and all-year reconstruction by 3.7 percent (trend RMSE 0.446 versus 0.170 degrees C per decade). Under empirical gap calendars the corresponding inflations were 1.8 and 0.5 percent, both small relative to the median classical slope standard error of 0.59 degrees C per decade. The paper also identifies where recovery fails: air-only reconstruction without local water information undercovers hidden peaks in regulated Bavarian reaches.

The software contribution is gapbench, an MIT-licensed Python package with public primitives for observed-window bounds, finite-record slopes, calendar-only year selection, slope-error decomposition, group-disjoint splits and archive masking, shipped with tests and a synthetic quickstart. It is available in the public repository https://github.com/shutiao23/stream-recoverability (release v1.2.0); reviewers can access and run it without credentials.

Limitations, including the absence of a locked new-station confirmation, the diagnostic (non-causal) role of the flow analysis, and data-access conditions, are stated in the manuscript itself rather than repeated here.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere.
