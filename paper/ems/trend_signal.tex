\noindent\textbf{Interpretation.} The complete-record reference slope is itself weakly constrained: across 41 stations and a median of 14 reference years, the median OLS H30 slope is 0.754 $^{\circ}$C/10a, its median classical SE is 0.590 $^{\circ}$C/10a, and the median interannual H30 SD is 1.011 $^{\circ}$C; only 3 stations have OLS $p<0.05$ and 4 have tie-corrected Mann--Kendall $p<0.05$. Under empirical gap calendars, warm-season screening and all-year reconstruction give RMSEs of 0.114 and 0.067 $^{\circ}$C/10a, both well below the median SE (0.590 $^{\circ}$C/10a); the median $|e|/$SE is 0.002 (P90 0.279) for screening and 0.000 (P90 0.095) for reconstruction, with 3 versus 0 sign flips. Under controlled 30--90-day gaps, reconstruction reduces RMSE from 0.446 to 0.170 $^{\circ}$C/10a, the share of archives exceeding one SE from 13.42\% to 0.75\%, and sign flips from 53 to 16. The 0.446 $^{\circ}$C/10a screening RMSE is 59.1\% of the median reference slope (0.754 $^{\circ}$C/10a), but this is an aggregate error-to-slope ratio, not a per-archive relative underestimate: the median per-archive $|e|/$SE is 0.357 and 13.42\% of archives exceed one SE. Because the OLS SE excludes autocorrelation, the error/SE ratios are lower bounds on the trend's sampling uncertainty.
