\subsection{Algebraic year-set error and an iid working benchmark}

For a fixed station, let $F=Y_i$ be the complete reference-year set and let $S$ be a retained subset that contains every year of $F$ outside the masked years. Write the annual H30 vector as $h$, with entries ordered by year. The ordinary-least-squares slope weights in decade units are
\[
 w_{F,y}=\frac{x_y}{\sum_{z\in F}x_z^2},\qquad x_y=\frac{y-\bar y_F}{10},
\]
and $w_{S,y}=w_{F,y}$ for $y\in S$ and $w_{S,y}=0$ otherwise. The finite-record target is $\beta_F=w_F^\top h$ and the selected-set slope is $\beta_S=w_S^\top h$. Their difference is
\begin{equation}
 S_{\mathrm{set}}=(w_S-w_F)^\top h=(w_S-w_F)^\top e,
 \label{eq:year-set-identity}
\end{equation}
where $e=h-\bar h_F\mathbb{1}-\hat b_F x$ is the residual from the complete-reference linear fit. The second equality is exact because the weight difference sums to zero and is orthogonal to the year vector $x$. Thus a constant shift and a strictly linear trend cancel: if the annual sequence were exactly linear and recovery introduced no error, every retained subset would give the same slope. The observed year-set error is the inner product of the weight difference with the residual, not with the fitted line.

Under the additional working model $h=\alpha\mathbb{1}+bx+\varepsilon$ with $\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon)=\sigma^2 I$ and a year set fixed independently of $\varepsilon$,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Var}(S_{\mathrm{set}})
 =\sigma^2\left(\frac{1}{S_{xx,S}}-\frac{1}{S_{xx,F}}\right),
 \qquad S_{xx,A}=\sum_{y\in A}x_y^2 .
 \label{eq:year-set-variance}
\end{equation}
This follows from \eqref{eq:year-set-identity} and the orthogonality of the weight difference to the intercept and slope. Within that working model, maximizing $S_{xx,S}$ minimizes this variance term, which is why the $S_{xx}$ rule is an interpretable baseline rather than an arbitrary heuristic.

Equation~\eqref{eq:year-set-identity} is algebraic. Equation~\eqref{eq:year-set-variance} is a model-based benchmark, not a coverage guarantee for the observed archive. The fitted residual $e$ can contain real interannual variability, curvature, changing observation support and measurement error; it is not all noise. The benchmark also conditions on the fixed year set and does not represent selection that depends on outcomes or on a different error structure. Table~\ref{tab:retention-geometry} compares the observed year-set RMSE with this benchmark. The two are of similar order in both gap families, so the magnitude of the observed effect alone does not identify a unique thermal mechanism or assign a percentage of the error to geometry. The contribution is the measured consequence of screening, selection and recovery in the river archive, not the general least-squares identity.
