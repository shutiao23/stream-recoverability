Dear Editors of Water Resources Research,

Please consider our manuscript, “Retaining incomplete river-temperature records: time support of finite-record heat slopes.”

Observed 30-day windows show that strict complete-year screening can discard reported sustained-heat facts. Most witnesses pass a warm-season screen; two excluded windows exceed that screen's accepted record. The slope target is defined on each station's fixed set of complete reference years, which need not be contiguous. On 805 forward controlled-gap archives with 30–90-day gaps, restoring all supported years improves the finite-record H30 slope. Exact enumeration and stronger calendar-based same-count controls show that year choice improves that slope at a fixed count; restoring all supported years improves it further. An empirical-calendar extension on a separate 818-archive cohort gives a more mixed result: the CDF interval includes zero, whereas the Sxx and all-year intervals do not. These comparisons do not isolate a pure year-count effect or a missingness mechanism.

Hidden-peak undercoverage concentrates in Bavarian air-supported reconstructions. Common geometric weighting retains a regional contrast, but support loss and training-history imbalance prevent causal attribution. We also regenerate 2,370 original source-calibration cases across three nested trajectory seeds and budgets and score the same 27 target station-years. Re-estimated source offsets change endpoints in both regional strata and local method comparisons; 500 paths do not establish convergence or general method superiority. We report D2's broader point-estimate availability separately from its accuracy: it is available on all 820 forward archives, while the classical comparator is available on 738 and 701; this does not establish a method winner or a releasable value. Negative release results and external-reference limits remain documented in the Supporting Information.
