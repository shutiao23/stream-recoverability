Dear Editors of Water Resources Research,

Please consider our manuscript, “Retaining incomplete river-temperature records: time support of finite-record heat slopes.”

The central question is what a heat-slope estimate fitted to incomplete years can support. On 805 forward controlled-gap archives with 30–90-day gaps, restoring all supported years reduces finite-record H30 slope RMSE from 0.446 to 0.170 °C per decade. On a separate 818-archive empirical-calendar cohort the reduction is from 0.114 to 0.067; there, the CDF year-distribution selector's paired RMSE-difference interval includes zero. These are cohort summaries, not per-archive guarantees: all-year recovery has a larger absolute slope error in 169 of 805 controlled-gap archives and 57 of 818 calendar archives, while 530 of the 818 calendar archives retain the same year set and show no change. The target is a fixed, station-specific set of complete reference years, which need not be contiguous, and the paper claims neither a universal method winner nor a physical mechanism.

Observed 30-day windows show that strict complete-year screening can discard reported sustained-heat facts. Most witnesses pass a warm-season screen; two excluded windows exceed that screen's accepted record. Year choice can improve the finite-record slope at a fixed count, and restoring all supported years improves it further, but the empirical-calendar result is more mixed. Hidden-peak undercoverage concentrates in Bavarian air-supported reconstructions; common geometric weighting retains a regional contrast, but support loss and training-history imbalance prevent causal attribution.

We also regenerate original source-calibration cases across nested trajectory seeds and budgets on the same 27 target station-years. Re-estimated source offsets change endpoints in both regional strata and local method comparisons; 500 paths establish neither convergence nor general method superiority. We report the broader point-estimate availability of the distributional method separately from its accuracy, and we do not treat availability as a releasable value. Negative release results and external-reference limits remain documented in the Supporting Information.
