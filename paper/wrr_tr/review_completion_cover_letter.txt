Dear Editors of Water Resources Research,

Please consider our manuscript, “Retaining incomplete river-temperature records: temporal support and uncertainty in sustained-heat trends.”

Observed 30-day windows show that strict complete-year screening can discard reported sustained-heat facts. Most witnesses pass a warm-season screen; two excluded windows exceed that screen's accepted record. Jointly degraded archives show that restoring all supported years improves the specified finite-record slope. Exact enumeration and stronger calendar-based same-count controls show that year choice improves the finite-record slope at a fixed count; restoring all supported years improves it further. These comparisons do not isolate a pure year-count effect.

Hidden-peak undercoverage concentrates in Bavarian air-supported reconstructions. Common geometric weighting retains a regional contrast, but support loss and training-history imbalance prevent causal attribution. We also regenerate 2,370 original source-calibration cases across three nested trajectory seeds and budgets and score the same 27 target station-years. Re-estimated source offsets change endpoints in both regional strata and local method comparisons; 500 paths do not establish convergence or general method superiority. Negative release results and external-reference limits remain documented in the Supporting Information.
