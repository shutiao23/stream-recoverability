\subsection{Empirical-calendar extension and selection opportunity}

The calendar extension applies the same three outcome-blind rules to the saved forward empirical-calendar predictions. It does not refit a daily or annual model, change the source calibration or alter the fixed complete years. There are \SRCalendarWarmTruthRMSE{} $^{\circ}$C per decade of year-set error under warm-season screening and \SRCalendarWarmRMSE{} total estimated-slope RMSE on the 818 actual-mask archives. The CDF rule gives \SRCalendarCDFRMSE{}, the $S_{xx}$ rule gives \SRCalendarSxxRMSE{}, and retaining every supported year gives \SRCalendarAllRMSE{}. Uniform exact averaging gives \SRCalendarUniformRMSE{}. The corresponding comparator-minus-warm-season paired intervals are \SRCalendarCDFDifference{} [\SRCalendarCDFLow, \SRCalendarCDFHigh] for CDF, \SRCalendarSxxDifference{} [\SRCalendarSxxLow, \SRCalendarSxxHigh] for $S_{xx}$, and \SRCalendarAllDifference{} [\SRCalendarAllLow, \SRCalendarAllHigh] for all years. The CDF interval includes zero; the $S_{xx}$ and all-year intervals lie below zero in this cohort.

The opportunity to select years differs from the control-gap family. Only \SRCalendarChoiceN{} of the 818 empirical-calendar archives offer a choice between different same-count subsets; \SRCalendarAllKeptN{} already retain every incomplete year and \SRCalendarNoneKeptN{} retain none. The extension therefore measures a narrower selection margin, and it is not an external accuracy validation on genuinely unknown gaps. The control-gap and empirical-calendar cohorts are also not exchangeable: their rows are separate, and subtracting their differences would not identify a causal effect of a missingness mechanism. Within the calendar family, sign disagreement with the complete-reference slope is \SRCalendarSignWarm/818 under warm-season screening, \SRCalendarSignCDF/818 under CDF, \SRCalendarSignSxx/818 under $S_{xx}$ and \SRCalendarSignAll/818 when all supported years are retained. These counts are small and describe this cohort only. They show that aggregate RMSE and direction agreement are different criteria, not that any rule is a safe climate-direction classifier.
