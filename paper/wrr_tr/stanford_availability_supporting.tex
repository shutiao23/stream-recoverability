\subsection{Point-estimate accuracy and availability are different questions}

The paired accuracy table in the main text is restricted to cases where both D2 and the comparator have a finite forward point estimate under all-year retention. In the controlled-gap family that common set has \SRCrossedPairN{} archives; D2 RMSE is \SRCrossedDTwoRMSE{} and the classical comparator RMSE is \SRCrossedClassicRMSE{} $^{\circ}$C per decade, with a paired D2-minus-classical interval of [\SRCrossedPairLow, \SRCrossedPairHigh]. In the empirical-calendar family the common set has \SRCalendarPairN{} archives; the corresponding values are \SRCalendarDTwoRMSE{}, \SRCalendarClassicRMSE{} and [\SRCalendarPairLow, \SRCalendarPairHigh]. The controlled-gap interval lies above zero, while the calendar interval includes zero. These are fixed-budget point-estimate comparisons: they do not establish a universal accuracy ranking, and they do not describe probability-interval availability.

Availability has a different denominator. D2 supplies a finite point estimate on \SRAvailDTwoCrossed{} forward controlled-gap archives and on \SRAvailDTwoCalendar{} forward empirical-calendar archives. The classical comparator supplies one on \SRAvailClassicCrossed{} and \SRAvailClassicCalendar{}, respectively. On the \SRAvailCrossedMissingN{} controlled-gap and \SRAvailCalendarMissingN{} calendar archives where the classical comparator is unavailable but D2 is available, D2 RMSE is \SRAvailCrossedMissingRMSE{} and \SRAvailCalendarMissingRMSE{} $^{\circ}$C per decade. A larger availability denominator therefore does not imply better accuracy on the common cases, and it does not certify that the additional values are suitable for release. Missing classical outputs remain unavailable; they are not assigned zero error. These counts are for the all-year point-estimate layer and must not be substituted for the 805/818 retention-rule cohorts or for probability-interval coverage denominators.
