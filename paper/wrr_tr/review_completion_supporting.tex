\section{Design and information separation}
The source comparison uses twenty repetitions of masks and group-role assignment on the same assembled archive; these are not independent climates. Held test connected groups are excluded from estimator fitting, interval calibration, risk learning and rule calibration. In the original partitioned forward models, other training-only groups in a named target region can remain in source roles. The source-role audit and subsequent strict-region sensitivities distinguish this exception from the held target-group exclusion.

In each whole-archive setting, selected gaps in the target station are masked together before local daily fitting. Unmasked target observations and the designated donor observations remain available. The source annual model is fitted separately and held fixed within that archive reanalysis. The first seven years are observed burn-in; the forward evaluation masks only years from 2010 onward. Twenty repetitions at the same station are not twenty independent rivers. Initial seven-station stress pilots are followed by a 41-station forward sensitivity; the heat-associated extension fixes the number of missing years but can change their dates and the number of missing days.

\section{Observation-rule accounting}
The rule table uses every eligible station-year, not only the selected witnesses. A reported window is not a complete-year H30, and an accepted incomplete year can contain an unobserved higher peak. The ``new facts'' column therefore compares reported windows with the maximum reported window among the other accepted years; it does not prove a full-record ordering. The measurement-margin sensitivity treats a window ordering as unresolved when the reported difference is at most twice the assumed absolute error on each window. These margins are assumptions, not sensor-error estimates.


\section{Joint-rank numerical convergence}
The convergence check fixes the annual quantiles, observed bounds and evaluation years and increases the stratified sample count through 256, 1,024 and 4,096 on the difficult forward controlled gaps. Three random seeds index the rank construction. The rank sequence is generated at 4,096 draws and truncated for smaller counts; annual marginals are regenerated separately for each sample size and remain specific to each estimator. Coverage, width and interval score are reported with the across-seed range. The range is not a confidence interval, and the check uses the same opened data as the main analysis. A 0.3-point coverage rule and 1\% width and interval-score rules are applied to the three-seed mean. Five of six method--construction pairs meet the coverage rule on that mean. The largest three-seed-mean coverage change is \CCRankChangeMax{} percentage points, and the largest single-seed change is \CCRankSeedChangeMax{} percentage points. Averaging seeds can cancel opposing changes, so these rules are not a resolution guarantee and the constructions are not ranked. At 4,096 samples, nominal 95\% trend coverage remains 87.4--89.9\%.

\section{Scoring and interval construction}
For ensemble $x_1,\ldots,x_M$ and outcome $y$, the continuous ranked probability score is
\[ M^{-1}\sum_m|x_m-y|-(2M^2)^{-1}\sum_{m,l}|x_m-x_l|. \]
Threshold-weighted scoring replaces samples and outcome by their maxima with the training H30 threshold. The 90\% interval score adds twenty times the distance outside the interval to its width. A 95\% trend interval score uses the corresponding factor forty. Missing predictions remain unavailable rather than being assigned zero error.

Both annual estimators use the observed-window support projection where a finite bound exists. The projection is applied to the annual distribution and then carried into the slope; a trend interval is not projected onto an annual lower bound. Seven stored quantiles do not determine their outer tails; the normal-spacing continuation and the alternative constant tail are assumptions. Within one estimator, cross-year rank constructions keep that estimator's annual marginal samples fixed. The two estimators have different annual marginals. The constructions are not a posterior for the fitted annual model. The source-only corrections and the post-hoc propagation reported below do not establish a finite-sample or independent-climate coverage guarantee.

In whole-archive annual coverage and the peak-visibility analysis, inclusive endpoint comparisons use an absolute $10^{-8}\,^{\circ}$C numerical tolerance. After detecting boundary sensitivity in the USGS exploration, we compared this convention with exact floating-point comparisons and a $10^{-10}\,^{\circ}$C tolerance on the same saved two-estimator common cases. For the forward empirical-calendar subset, D2 covers 3,610 of 3,674 repeated masked cases with either tolerance, but an exact comparison covers 2,037; all 1,573 changed comparisons lie in the reference-at-observed-bound stratum. Across all four family--mode combinations and both estimators, the two nonzero tolerances agree on every common-case classification. An independent scalar audit found the largest strict-comparison endpoint gap among changed method--case comparisons was $8.18\times10^{-14}\,^{\circ}$C. These tolerances concern arithmetic on the reported target, not physical measurement uncertainty, and masks from a station-year are not independent. The pooled common-case comparison differs in weighting and availability from the equal-archive means used elsewhere; no original saved coverage was replaced.

The historical single-year source comparison also uses an absolute $10^{-8}\,^{\circ}$C endpoint tolerance after support projection. A post-hoc check reproduced the published coverage for all 20 saved source repetitions, two methods, two gap families and both overlap strata. In the controlled-gap, high-overlap stratum, $10^{-10}$ and $10^{-8}\,^{\circ}$C give identical coverage classifications and median D2 and stochastic coverage remains 89.3\% and 89.8\%, respectively. Outside the high-overlap stratum there are 14 repeated method--case comparisons where the two tolerances differ, with endpoint gaps between $1.89\times10^{-9}$ and $9.93\times10^{-9}\,^{\circ}$C. An independent scalar implementation reproduced the 160 method--family--stratum--seed counts. These are the same historical source predictions, not independent sensor validation or a population calibration guarantee.

For the exploratory release-then-trend analysis, an observed lower bound $L$ contributes $\log\Phi\{(\mu-L)/\sigma\}$ to a Gaussian working likelihood. Released numerical estimates are treated as exact, unknown cases remain unavailable, and the working interval includes regression variability. It is not a calibrated missingness-only interval.

\section{Additional reservoir-network reference qualification}
After the preceding external searches, we examined a 2026 HydroShare collection of continuous water-quality observations above and below reservoirs in Colorado and New Mexico (Van Horn et al., \url{https://www.hydroshare.org/resource/430ee557554948b5811830c1e2ec41aa/}). One of eleven resources named a previously contacted USGS gage; ten remaining candidate resources were retained for screening, including those with possible network overlap. For the eleven referenced gage proxies, 200-km upstream and downstream NLDI searches found previously named sites for six gages and source-panel sites for two. Gages are proxies rather than necessarily the sensor locations, and a bounded search without a hit does not establish independence.

The ten published level-1 files contained 69 station-years. Reported fixed Mountain Standard Time labels allowed 29 potentially complete calendar years before checking numerical values. Requiring exactly 96 unique quarter-hour values per day, all finite and in the broad $-5$ to $50\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ sensor-range guard, yielded 16,153 complete days but no complete calendar year. This is a screen of these ten files under this stringent rule, not a statement about all years in the network. The published level-1 process can correct measurements, while explicit per-value correction or estimation flags were not found; finite values do not establish independent, uncorrected ground truth. No annual H30, model error, or trend was scored. The candidate package includes attributed time-and-temperature column extracts, station-year counts, and an independent replay. USGS series metadata for four of eleven gages show some water-temperature records, including instantaneous records extending into the study period for two Arkansas gages, but contemporaneous coverage and co-location remain unverified and both gages have known source-panel network connections.

After inspecting the completeness results, we bounded H30 in every 2021--2025 station-year, assuming each unobserved or ambiguous quarter-hour lies between $-5$ and $50\,^{\circ}$C and treating finite, uniquely reported values as fixed. The range is an assumption about unobserved water, not an established physical or sensor-error bound. Each daily bound is the mean after setting all unknown slots to the relevant endpoint; the annual bound is the maximum of the resulting 30-day moving averages wholly inside the calendar year. The construction encloses the reported-series target only if the assumptions hold. Four of 50 intervals have width at most 0.01 $^{\circ}$C and 13 at most 0.1 $^{\circ}$C, whereas the median width is 25.18 $^{\circ}$C. The ten sites are not independent climates and the narrow intervals were selected after the availability study; no H30 prediction was scored.

All 50 years contain a fully observed 30-day window. A generic 95\% complete-day rule accepts 11 years and rejects 39 with visible windows; the 100\% rule accepts none. Among rejected years with at least one previously accepted year during 2021--2023, one has a visible window exceeding every previously accepted annual upper bound. At Rio Chama below Abiquiu, 2024 has 346/366 complete days, and its visible 30-day mean is 19.386 $^{\circ}$C. The only earlier accepted year in 2021--2023 is 2022, with 357/365 complete days and conditional H30 interval [18.461, 18.461] $^{\circ}$C; the 2024 window is 0.925 $^{\circ}$C higher. The 2024 conditional upper bound is 28.190 $^{\circ}$C, and rejected 2021 or 2023 may also have higher H30. This is not a record against all earlier years, a physical measurement-error guarantee, or a prevalence estimate. Source quality corrections and hydrologic dependence remain unresolved. An independent implementation recomputes all 50 bounds and both screening rules; the candidate package replays them from attributed source extracts.

\subsection{Partial-history exploratory model comparison}
The original model pipeline requires at least four complete daily training years, which none of these sites supplies. After examining completeness and H30 bounds, we fixed an exploratory adaptation using all candidate target years with at least 30 complete days and at least three other history years satisfying 75\% complete days, observed endpoints, and no missing run longer than 45 days. Missing days in each selected history year were linearly interpolated within that year; up to 58 days in one history year were interpolated across these cases. The target year was excluded from fitting, and its naturally incomplete days and additional controlled 30/60/90-day masks were hidden together. We supplied the actual count of complete training years, zero, to the fixed global source model and source-only calibration. This is outside the original complete-history regime, not a strict replay of it. NASA POWER local-solar-time air forcing may not align with the reported fixed-MST water days; no neighboring water donor was used. Prior network exposure is known for some gage proxies, while no 200-km hit for others does not prove independence.

All 306 masks in 17 target station-years at four sites produced all three point estimates. For a point $p$ and a conditional reference interval $[L,U]$, the absolute error is bounded by $\max(L-p,0,p-U)$ and $\max(|p-L|,|p-U|)$. Predictive coverage is definite only if the interval contains $[L,U]$, and possible when the intervals intersect. On the post-hoc subset of 180 masks in ten station-years at three sites with reference width at most 0.1 $^{\circ}$C, mean absolute error is bounded by [0.299, 0.325] for plug-in, [0.348, 0.374] for D2 and [0.318, 0.343] $^{\circ}$C for the calibrated stochastic comparator. The paired D2-minus-plug-in mean absolute-error difference is bounded by [0.045, 0.052] $^{\circ}$C; the D2-minus-stochastic difference is [0.026, 0.033] $^{\circ}$C. These average per-case extrema are conservative because masks within a station-year share an unknown H30. D2 nominal 90\% coverage is bounded between 80.0\% and 88.9\%, and stochastic coverage between 83.9\% and 95.6\%, on that same selected set. The full 306-case cohort has mean absolute-error upper bounds exceeding 11 $^{\circ}$C for all methods, so its rankings remain weakly identified. These are repeated masks, not independent sites, and no general ranking or coverage guarantee follows.

An independent scalar implementation recomputed all bounded scores. In one selected mask per target year, paired same-process perturbations of all hidden observed daily values and of natural-gap placeholders preserved all 68 model features and 17 outputs, within $10^{-10}$ tolerance. An earlier comparison against saved outputs failed in four of 17 masks under the audit's default numerical-thread environment, with maximum drift 0.0061 $^{\circ}$C; setting OMP, OpenBLAS, MKL and NumExpr threads to one before numerical imports yielded agreement in those 17 checks. An additional single-thread replay reproduced all 306 saved masks, features and predictions using the same code and source model. This does not prove that every mask is free of leakage, quantify physical uncertainty, or independently refit the restricted model.

\subsection{Training-feature support and the numerical gate}
After the score analysis, we reconstructed the global model's seed-zero role assignment and bootstrap weights. Its quantile regressors fitted 43,804 distinct positive-weight source cases from 68 recorded groups, with total bootstrap weight 68,028. One-dimensional finite training minima and maxima were compared with all 68 decision-time input features. All 306 reservoir cases have zero complete training years, below the source estimator range of 3--41. Excluding this feature, every case is still outside a training extremum: 270 have a training-anomaly lag-one correlation below the estimator minimum and the remaining 36 have a reconstruction-residual lag-one correlation above its maximum. These are repeated masks in 17 station-years. For comparison, 1,931 of 30,384 crossed-mask cases in positive-weight non-estimator source roles are outside a marginal training range excluding complete-year count; those source cases were used in other model components and are not a new independent test set. Ranges are marginal, not a multivariate support guarantee, and this comparison does not establish a failure probability.

For the 17 reservoir station-years, the anomaly correlation calculated from interpolated historical days is outside the source range in 15 years. Rebuilding the historical climatology using only fully observed days, while retaining the interpolated version's warm months, leaves 14 of those 17 correlations outside the source range. This changes both the climatology and finite lag pairs, so it does not isolate a causal interpolation effect; it also does not recompute the residual correlation or prediction accuracy. An independent day-of-year and scalar correlation implementation reproduced these results.

For a separate diagnostic, we counterfactually clipped complete-year count, anomaly correlation, and residual correlation to their respective source training extrema, individually and jointly. All 306 cases retained the same leaf IDs in all seven quantile regressors and the classifier, and hence the same raw quantile outputs and classifier scores. Sorting, source offset and observed-window projection were independently reproduced. This does not mean the features have no influence inside the training range, and clipping an actually incomplete history is not a valid fix. The original three-output rule additionally requires at least four complete history years, so it cannot release a numerical estimate for any of these cases regardless of the individual score. If quality flags were all absent and optional limits were omitted, 304 cases would produce an observed bound and two would be unknown; actual source quality flags are unresolved, so those are conditional states, not actual releases. An individual score at or below 0.1 occurred in 158 cases, but it is not a group-risk guarantee.

\subsection{Source evaluation of a marginal-range warning}
After inspecting external cases and source outcomes, we examined whether the marginal-range warning identifies errors in crossed-mask cases from the global model's positive-weight rule-calibration role. That role is group-disjoint from the quantile estimator, interval calibration and risk learning roles, but was used in other rule analyses. We retained 8,010 cases from 19 groups with finite D2, reference and individual score, a finite observed bound and at least four complete historical years; 648 of 8,658 source role cases did not meet these criteria. Quality flags and the original optional range gates were not applied, so this is not the complete three-output policy. An error is an absolute D2 error above 0.5 $^{\circ}$C against the reported complete reference.

The warning flags an input outside any estimator-role marginal minimum/maximum, excluding complete-year count, or a nonfinite category absent in training. It flags 484 cases, including 42 of 868 errors: 4.8\% recall and 6.2\% false-positive rate among non-errors. Score $\leq0.1$ alone retains 6,007 cases with 288 errors. Requiring both score $\leq0.1$ and no warning retains 5,596 with 268 errors. To compare equal numerical retention, an outcome-free but transductive ranking of the same cases by score retains 5,596 with 211 errors. The case-weighted difference in conditional failure is $+1.02$ percentage points; the 19-group bootstrap interval is [+0.68, +1.41] points. Selected years can differ, and the score-only cutoff is selected from this evaluated cohort's score distribution, so neither the interval nor the matched comparison is an external safety guarantee.

As a post-hoc numerical-tolerance sensitivity, we enlarged each finite training range by 0.000001 or 0.01 of that feature's weighted 1st--99th percentile span, falling back to its full range or one for zero span. The smaller margin leaves the counts unchanged. At the 0.01 margin, the warning flags 430 source cases and 38 errors (4.4\% recall); the same-count score-plus-warning and score-only policies retain 268 and 216 errors in 5,620 cases, respectively. It flags 270 of the 306 reservoir masks, rather than all 306. Margins were chosen after earlier results, source cases are repeated masks, and one-dimensional ranges do not establish multivariate support. Independent Booster, decision, group and bootstrap implementations reproduced both comparisons.

\subsection{Source-history degradation and count-feature sensitivity}
After inspecting the reservoir results, we selected all 15 Swiss and Bavarian stations from the earlier processed-data cohort with complete daily years 2009--2017: 11 Swiss and four Bavarian stations, representing six recorded connected groups. The target is the reported 2017 calendar-year H30. Its nine fixed target masks cross 30/60/90-day lengths with three calendar positions and remain identical across all interventions. In the full-history condition, 2009--2016 daily observations are retained. In the degraded-history condition, July 1--30 is hidden in each of those eight training years and linearly interpolated within that year before fitting the local daily model. The fixed source annual models and calibration exclude target-region groups; no water donor is used.

The D2 complete-history-count feature is crossed with history condition at values 0 and 8. A count of 8 is truthful only for the full history; zero is truthful only for the degraded history. The two other combinations deliberately misstate history completeness and are diagnostic counterfactuals, not deployment rules. In the 135 cases common to all four D2 combinations and both plug-in and stochastic history versions, D2 MAE is 0.158 $^{\circ}$C for full/count 8 and 0.188 $^{\circ}$C for degraded/count 0. Averaging over the two count settings gives a case-equal history effect of $+0.027\,^{\circ}$C; averaging over histories gives a count-8-minus-count-0 effect of $-0.002\,^{\circ}$C. The corresponding six-group-equal estimates are $+0.042\,^{\circ}$C and approximately zero, with 4,000 group-bootstrap intervals [$-0.009$, 0.092] and [$-0.0015$, 0.0014] $^{\circ}$C. D2 nominal 90\% coverage changes from 91.1\% for full/count 8 to 88.9\% for degraded/count 0, while mean interval score changes from 0.787 to 0.963 $^{\circ}$C. Repeated masks and clustered stations do not provide 135 independent tests.

The selected years, data vintage and source results were previously examined; a single fixed warm-season degradation and one target year do not characterize real missingness. They also cannot isolate why the reservoir adaptation differed from its comparators. Independent arithmetic recomputed the 15 target H30 values, all 540 factorial rows and six-group summaries; one post-hoc perturbation of 30 hidden target days and 240 hidden historical days left the corresponding model inputs and predictions unchanged. A single-thread replay in a separately provisioned Python environment reproduced all 540 saved masks, features and predictions using the same code and local restricted models. The open snapshot replay checks source water-year completeness and air inputs, but the source model and case predictions remain subject to separate access conditions.
\section{Previously opened USGS daily targets with continuous support}
After the preceding studies, we reexamined all ten previously opened USGS daily-value candidates (\url{https://api.waterdata.usgs.gov/}). Eight complete calendar years at four sites had finite approved daily means, no ambiguity, and no estimated qualifier recognized by the existing parser; every target had at least three other years with at least 75\% usable days, finite endpoints and no missing run longer than 45 days. Missing historical daily values were linearly interpolated within each history year, while the actual complete history count was zero to two. Target years were excluded from local fitting, but later history was allowed. These inputs fall outside the global model's estimator training range of three to 41 complete history years. The global source model and source-only calibration were held fixed, without a target water donor; this is not the original four-complete-history protocol or a prospective forecast.

A post-hoc audit checked all 2,922 raw published daily records in the eight target years: each was approved with an empty qualifier and matched the processed target value. Empty qualifiers do not prove absence of unrecorded corrections or physical measurement error. All four daily series have a uniquely matched continuous parent ID in the USGS metadata. We obtained the continuous parent observations for all eight target years and required finite, approved, unique, quarter-hour-grid readings with no nonempty qualifier. Under fixed local standard time, three years have at least one accepted reading in each of the 24 physical hours of every day, and one of those years has all 15-minute slots; all three years are at Rio Mora near Terrero, New Mexico (USGS 08377900). Under local civil time, daylight-saving transition days have 23 or 25 physical hours; an independent scalar audit verified 5,844 day-by-clock records across both conventions. One of the four target station IDs is present in a source panel; this alone does not prove positive-weight source-model use. A bounded 200-km network search found no named source or other prior station for Rio Mora, but a non-hit does not prove an independent watershed or climate. Daily records and some target H30 outcomes had already been examined; selection is post-hoc.

The reported daily H30 in Rio Mora in 2017 is 14.1100 $^{\circ}$C. Recomputing from the fully supported 15-minute series gives 14.1066 $^{\circ}$C using fixed standard time and 14.1055 $^{\circ}$C using civil time. Close annual values do not establish the actual reporting clock, physical sensor accuracy, unrecorded corrections, or USGS daily preprocessing. The station's earlier quality report also flagged possible ice effects and long constant runs. NASA POWER air forcing uses local solar time and need not align with either water-day convention.

All 144 artificial masks have finite plug-in, D2 and calibrated stochastic point estimates. Against the reported complete daily reference, their MAEs are 0.304, 0.251 and 0.300 $^{\circ}$C, respectively; nominal 90\% coverage is 121/144 for D2 and 123/144 for the stochastic method. In the post-hoc fixed-standard hourly-supported subset, all 54 masks are drawn from three years at the single Rio Mora station: MAEs are 0.266, 0.180 and 0.174 $^{\circ}$C, with nominal interval coverage 52/54 and 54/54. Repeated masks do not make these independent stations or climates; no regional accuracy, trend or risk guarantee follows.

A subsequent audit found that the existing daily parser recognized estimated values but retained other nonempty qualifiers. In Rio Mora, 2015 and 2016 contain 57 and 88 raw daily ICE qualifiers, respectively; ICE does not itself prove the values incorrect. Conservatively removing every nonempty daily qualifier and reapplying the same history eligibility rule removes both years from each of the three Rio Mora target histories. Across all eight target years, the same 144 original masks remain supported; five targets have unchanged histories. On the Rio Mora 54 masks the plug-in, D2 and stochastic MAEs change from 0.266, 0.180 and 0.174 to 0.242, 0.226 and 0.218 $^{\circ}$C, respectively. This post-hoc comparison simultaneously changes input values and historical sample size. A scalar audit of raw qualifiers and annual eligibility passed. A fixed-mask hidden-value perturbation on one mask in each of the eight target years and an isolated single-thread replay of all 144 qualifier-aware cases matched saved inputs and outputs; neither is an exhaustive leakage proof.

The saved reported-daily H30 and an independently summed version differ by at most $7.11\times10^{-15}\,^{\circ}$C, yet intervals often touch an observed lower bound, so exact floating-point comparisons change up to 27 of 144 coverage classifications across these arithmetic paths. A post-hoc absolute $10^{-10}\,^{\circ}$C comparison tolerance yields identical classifications for the two paths. For Rio Mora, original-history D2 and stochastic reported-daily coverages are 53/54 and 54/54 with this tolerance, versus 52/54 and 54/54 using the saved exact comparison. With qualifier-aware histories, the tolerant coverages are 48/54 and 53/54. This numerical convention is not a measurement-error model. An independent scalar audit verified all 24 coverage summary cells.

The 144 cases have zero, one or two genuinely complete training histories. All are below the estimator-role minimum of three, although the other 67 inputs lie within their individual source extrema; marginal inclusion does not establish joint support. Replacing the history count by the training minimum is knowingly false and leaves every quantile-model and classifier leaf path unchanged, so it is not a correction. The original numerical-release gate requires four complete history years and therefore cannot release numeric values for these cases. An independent scoring audit checked all 144 saved outputs against reported daily references; eight same-process hidden-value probes and an isolated single-thread replay of all masks and model inputs passed. The open USGS/NASA snapshot independently replays reference arithmetic and clock qualification, but omits the restricted source weights and per-case predictions.

\subsection{Reporting resolution and source-recalibrated support margins}
We subsequently bounded the annual 15-minute-sample H30 in all eight previously opened USGS years under both fixed-standard and civil reporting clocks. Each missing, flagged, duplicated or otherwise ambiguous quarter-hour value is assumed to lie in $[-5,50]\,^{\circ}$C; an off-grid day is treated as entirely unknown. For each reporting day, lower and upper means assign all unknown slots to the corresponding endpoint; each annual bound is the maximum of its 30-day rolling sequence. This hypothetical range is not a validated instrument or physical-water limit. Under fixed standard time, three years have zero-width H30 bounds, all at Rio Mora: 2012 and 2019 each retain a small number of unknown quarter-hours despite full hourly coverage, while 2017 has every quarter-hour slot. Independent UTC-slot and scalar-window code reproduced all 5,844 day-by-clock bounds.

On the 54 masks in those three years, nominal 90\% D2 coverage is 52/54 against published daily H30 but 33/54 against the fixed-standard continuous H30; stochastic coverage is 54/54 and 34/54. In 20 masks the largest observed published-day window exceeds the conditional continuous H30 upper bound; the D2 and stochastic 5th percentiles equal the published bound in all 20. The discrepancy has median 0.0034 and maximum 0.0118 $^{\circ}$C. All 21 D2 non-overlap distances are at most 0.05 $^{\circ}$C, with maximum 0.0203 $^{\circ}$C. This threshold was examined after the scores and is a descriptive resolution sensitivity, not a sensor-error estimate or calibrated coverage. Published daily and equally weighted quarter-hour targets can differ through clock, rounding or corrections; an apparent cross-target contradiction is not itself proof of model leakage or physical failure.

To examine the consequence of a measurement-error assumption, we applied support $L-\epsilon$ instead of the original published-window bound $L$ before and after quantile calibration, for both D2 and stochastic methods. For $\epsilon=0$, 0.01, 0.05 and 0.20 $^{\circ}$C, the source offsets were recomputed using the same positive-weight source interval-calibration roles: 13,007 cases from 13 recorded groups. Zero margin reproduces the previous source offsets and target quantiles. New target outcomes were not used in the recalculation, though prior source-panel contact is unresolved. The source calibration reference remains reported daily H30, so its validity for a latent continuous target is not established.

On the same 54 masks, all three positive margins yield 37/54 D2 and 34/54 stochastic continuous-target coverage; against the published daily target they yield 46/54 and 44/54. Under $\epsilon=0.05$, ten D2 lower interval endpoints and four medians fall below the original published observation bound. Thus a softened support changes the estimand or requires a justified measurement-error model; it cannot silently replace the exact reported-series constraint. Of 2,828 fixed-standard days with all quarter-hour samples, 264 have an absolute daily-mean discrepancy greater than 0.05 $^{\circ}$C; none exceeds 0.20 $^{\circ}$C in this observed subset. This does not bound missing days, validate either clock or establish instrument accuracy. A separate scalar implementation reproduced 868 source offset values and all 32 margin/clock/cohort/method summary cells. The experiment is post-hoc, uses one station for the three-year subset and does not establish reliable calibration or deployment safety.
