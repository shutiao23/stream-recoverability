\begin{abstract}
Screening incomplete river-temperature years changes the time support of sustained heat. We examine observed heat windows, finite-record slopes of the annual maximum 30-day mean (H30), and information conditions associated with recovery failures. Whole-archive evaluation uses 42 long complete records from a 581-station source archive. Observed windows in incomplete years can exceed every complete-year heat maximum; most witnesses also pass a warm-season rule. On 805 forward controlled-gap archives, recovering all supported years gives slope RMSE 0.170$^{\circ}$C per decade, versus 0.446 under warm-season screening. Two same-count controls that optimize calendar positions including fixed complete years reduce RMSE to 0.428 and 0.402, with paired group-resampling intervals supporting both improvements. Exact enumeration of random subsets gives 0.449; the earlier weak controls do not represent all same-count strategies. Hidden-peak 90\% coverage is 67.9\% for Bavarian air-ridge reconstructions. Common geometric weighting retains a regional contrast, but support loss and training-history imbalance prevent causal attribution. A trajectory-budget experiment regenerates 2,370 original source-calibration cases and scores the same 27 target station-years. Across three seeds, source-recalibrated median absolute 95th-percentile changes from 20 to 500 nested paths are 0.069--0.117$^{\circ}$C in the Swiss subset and 0.061--0.101$^{\circ}$C in Bavaria. Local method ordering also changes; 500 paths do not establish convergence or general superiority. These results support preserving observed heat facts and evaluating year selection, reconstruction and numerical uncertainty for a stated period.
\begin{plainlanguagesummary}
Gaps in a river's temperature record do not necessarily erase a hot year: thirty observed days can already be warmer than every complete year. Most such examples also pass a warm-season screen. In our experiments, the choice of years matters even when the number retained is fixed. Choosing years that better cover the record period reduces slope error, while restoring all supported years improves it further. Recovery failures concentrate in Bavarian air-supported reconstructions, but regional comparisons cannot identify their cause. More simulated paths and recalibration on the original independent source data change uncertainty endpoints and some method comparisons. These findings favor retaining observed heat facts and stating the temporal and information support of any reconstructed summary.
\end{plainlanguagesummary}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Stream-temperature records support studies of thermal habitat, climate change and water management \cite{caissie2006,michel2020,vanhamel2024}. The annual maximum of a 30-day mean (H30) summarizes sustained heat and is related to the thermal peak used in river-network studies \cite{beaufort2022}. A monitoring gap can change this statistic through the years it removes, the thermal states it hides, and the information that remains from other years or stations.

Prior work has already shown that partial stream-temperature records can support some annual analyses. \citeA{johnson2021} evaluated missing intervals and imputation for annual mean, phase and amplitude, finding limitations that varied among rivers. \citeA{beaufort2022} used summer observations and climate correction for a multiyear thermal-peak metric. Those results concern different targets: Johnson and coauthors do not supply a universal error baseline for annual H30, and Beaufort and coauthors do not evaluate jointly degraded multiyear archives. The question added here is how completeness screening changes the time support of sustained-heat information, and which observations and river-state information then leave annual recovery and trend propagation without support.

That question has two mechanisms. First, the years that a rule deletes, and the thermal states and time-leverage those years carry, change a slope fitted to the remaining finite record. Second, river thermal response, data processing and source support jointly determine the error of recovering a heat peak inside a gap. The intended use is a comparable finite-record H30 slope on a stated record period, together with fully observed 30-day windows retained as archive facts. If an observed lower bound already exceeds a pre-defined H30 threshold, that exceedance can be kept without a precise annual value. Study thresholds are not ecological-hazard standards, and a 0.5$^{\circ}$C reconstruction-error flag is not a calibrated probability at another threshold.

Regression record augmentation and maintenance-of-variance methods have a long hydrological history \cite{matalas1964,hirsch1982,vogel1985}. A point prediction, a predictive distribution and a decision to release a value have different targets. Distributional imputation should be assessed for the downstream quantity rather than only for daily squared error \cite{naf2023,gneiting2007}. We compare a daily plug-in, simple corrections, source-role-calibrated stochastic reconstruction and annual distribution regression, then jointly hide portions of a station's multiyear record before local refitting.

We organize the evidence around three research questions. RQ1: what observed heat information does each completeness rule retain or discard? RQ2: how do the retained year set and annual reconstruction errors combine in a slope for a specified record period? RQ3: under which visible information conditions do hidden-peak recovery errors concentrate? The regional, same-count and trajectory-budget diagnostics were developed after inspecting existing results; they are exploratory rather than preregistered confirmation. The six-station trajectory subset was fixed before that replay, not before the wider study. Its 0.05$^{\circ}$C tolerance is a numerical diagnostic, not a hydrological acceptance threshold. Table~\ref{tab:core-claims} states the corresponding evidence, denominators and boundaries. Throughout, \emph{calibrated} means an empirical offset estimated on disjoint source-role groups; it does not mean that a target region attains nominal coverage.

\section{Data and study design}
The source archive contains previously assembled European and US daily observations: 581 physical stations, 185 connected-record groups, 27 regions and 6,170 unique complete station-years. NASA POWER daily air temperature is the common meteorological input \cite{nasapower2026}. Artificial controlled gaps of 30, 60 or 90 days are crossed with overlap with a training-period climatological peak window; empirical incomplete calendars provide a separate family that is closer to the observed archive. Each of twenty source repetitions redraws masks and group-role composition. In the leave-one-year-out source comparison, fitting, interval calibration, score learning and rule calibration use disjoint connected groups outside each held region. In the original forward source split, HUC17 estimator and NVE interval-calibration roles retain other same-region groups, as detailed below. These repetitions describe variability within the available archive, not twenty independent climates. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} separates the observation-only, single-year and whole-archive evidence.

We use complete daily station-years as reference outcomes for artificial masking. Water observations hidden by a mask never enter the local fit for that case. Features available when a value is evaluated include the visible gap geometry, a training-period climatological peak, local water history, air conditions and available neighboring stations. The true peak location and hidden water values are evaluation quantities. Observation quality is inherited from the assembled source records; a reported value is not assumed to be error-free physical truth. An observed lower bound is valid for the reported series if its thirty measurements are comparable and valid.

The annual target is
\begin{equation}
 H30_y=\max_{j}\frac{1}{30}\sum_{d=j}^{j+29}Y_{yd}.
\end{equation}
H30 is used because it summarizes sustained heat. Native seven-day (H7) source refits exist in the repository; they are a different ecological window and are not substituted for H30 here. For an incomplete year, the largest fully observed 30-day mean $L_y$ satisfies $H30_y\ge L_y$ for the same reported series. For 1981--2025 we select stations with at least fifteen complete reference years and compare every incomplete-year bound with the largest complete-year H30. The observation-only denominator contains \CCEligibleYears{} eligible station-years at \CCEligibleStations{} stations in \CCEligibleGroups{} connected groups. We evaluate whether the same witnesses pass a 95\% annual-coverage rule and a 90\% station-specific warm-season rule. The five consecutive warm months are obtained from the station's climatology after excluding the evaluated year. These are explicit study comparators; the seven-week missing-period result of \citeA{johnson2021} concerns annual signal parameters, not a validated H30 record rule.

The whole-archive benchmark contains 42 stations with at least twenty complete reference years in seven regions, 18 connected groups and 3,320 station--repetition--setting evaluations (Figure~\ref{fig:sample-domain}; Table~\ref{tab:sample-domain}). One Finnish record ends in 1994, leaving 41 stations for forward evaluation. Twenty-four stations are in Bavaria and ten in Switzerland; that long-complete selection is a monitoring-network filter, not a random river sample. These 42 stations are the long-complete tail of the 581-station source archive: median complete years \PRArchiveTrendComplete{} versus \PRArchiveRestComplete{} among the remaining \PRArchiveRestN{} stations. Bavaria contributes \PRArchiveTrendBavaria{} of 42 evaluation stations against \PRArchiveRestBavaria{} of the remainder; USGS regions contribute \PRArchiveTrendUsgs{} versus \PRArchiveRestUsgs{} (Table~\ref{tab:archive-comparison}). Regulation metadata are unmatched\_gages or unknown for most sites and do not mean unregulated rivers; 35 of 42 evaluation stations are unmatched\_gages, five are labelled regulated and none unregulated. Daily values are provider daily means where that class is recorded. Visible-neighbour counts and process-metadata gaps are reported with the station roster. If area, elevation or discharge were complete they would be summarized; they are not. For the main retention contrast in the forward controlled-gap setting, both retention sets are available in \CCCohortCommon{} of \CCCohortAll{} settings; the other \CCCohortAll{} minus \CCCohortCommon{} settings are used only for availability accounting. The held-test-group main analysis excludes evaluated test groups from the named source roles. The forward HUC17 estimator and NVE interval calibration retain additional same-region training groups, so the strict-region sensitivity is reported separately. The 2010 split is a retrospective time condition on current-vintage records, not a general nonstationarity test. The saved historical \texttt{design.json} files are not rewritten; a correction notice accompanies them.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Annual recovery and the decision to release}
The daily plug-in computes H30 after a selected daily reconstruction. Functional debiasing corrects its annual value, whereas D2 learns annual-error quantiles using visible features and simulated trajectories. A stochastic regression comparator generates conditional residual bridges and calculates H30 for each realization. D2 and the stochastic comparator have the same source interval-calibration roles and bootstrap multiplicities. Point estimates are medians; MAE, CRPS, 90\% interval coverage and interval score are reported on available cases, with paired comparisons on common cases. Source-role calibration is an empirical offset on disjoint source groups; it is not a certificate that a target region attains nominal coverage.

In the twenty historical source fits, we apply the same observed-window support projection to both methods after their original calibration. The seven stored quantiles constrain only those seven probability levels. Values between them are interpolated, and the outer tails are extrapolated; for comparable CRPS integration we extend the tails using adjacent normal-quantile spacings and enforce $L_y$ when available. The original model weights and calibration offsets remain fixed in this source re-analysis. In the whole-archive study described below, offsets for both methods are instead re-estimated on their original independent calibration roles after projection. Stored stochastic bridge-repair tables are required to reproduce the historical source, open-source archive and group-maximum sensitivity calculations; these dependencies were checked retrospectively. Neither approach makes the assumed tail shape a verified full distribution.

The conditional-mean mechanism is useful but limited. For observed information $X$ and 30-day averaging vectors $w_j$, convexity gives
\[
 \max_j w_j^\top E(Y\mid X)\le E\{\max_j w_j^\top Y\mid X\}.
\]
It concerns the true conditional mean and conditional expectation; an approximate learner need not underestimate every individual year. Calibration, local adaptation, model bias and the chosen point summary remain empirical questions.

We evaluate a rule that reports an estimated value, an observed lower bound or an unknown state. Numerical release requires an available D2, at least four complete historical years, visible features within source-derived limits and a raw individual classifier score no greater than 0.10. The score was trained for a 0.5$^{\circ}$C absolute-error flag on the original unprojected D2; that flag is a reconstruction-tolerance diagnostic, not an ecological-hazard threshold, and the score is not a calibrated group-risk certificate for the projected value or for any other threshold. A seven-day close match between visible water and air temperature is tested for the imposed exact-air-substitution scenario; that detector is not evaluated for other stresses. No observed bound is released when this quality flag is raised. The primary factorial trend comparison is separate. A second exploratory analysis passes released values and bounds into a Gaussian working likelihood, treating released numerical estimates as exact and each bound $L$ as a right-censored response with contribution $P(H30\ge L)$. Unknown cases remain in availability denominators. The resulting regression interval includes working-model variability and is not a task-calibrated missingness-only interval. The three-output rule is retained as a boundary result, not as a proposed general publication system.

Two controlled-gap sensitivities assess missingness mechanisms on the first five masks at each of 41 forward-eligible stations. One hides the target's exact gaps from the donor panel; the other preferentially selects air-hot years while preserving the number of missing years. They are retrospective stresses rather than estimates of outage frequency. For the primary gap comparison, all sources use the annual longest contiguous missing run in 2010--2025. Empirical-calendar masks have median \PGRCalendarMedian{} days and \PGRCalendarLongThirty\% at least 30 days over \PGRCalendarN{} station-year--repetition rows; controlled masks have median \PGRControlledMedian{} days over \PGRControlledN{} rows, all at least 30 days. Observations receive one weight per station-year; trials receive one per station-year--repetition. These differing samples and repeat weights make this a descriptive comparison. The 1981--2025 observed summary is supplementary. The shared-year measure requires missingness at two connected stations in the same year, not on the same days. Of these, 38 share an incomplete year and 14 share at least one missing date; 40 of 2,086 evaluable missing station-days overlap another station. The other 35 station-years are excluded from this peer-conditioned denominator. This concerns the 41-station cohort, not every potential or selected model donor. Experimental year selection follows probability 0.35 after a seven-year burn-in; its frequency is not an observed outage estimate. Observation-only accounting uses 96 stations, whereas the whole-archive benchmark uses 42.

\subsection{Regional standardization, time-support controls and trajectory budget}
The Bavaria--Switzerland comparison reuses saved forward controlled-gap annual cases. Common-support screening retains bins of gap length, training-peak overlap, evaluation year and visible-donor count represented in both regions. It does not balance their frequencies. We therefore standardize the within-cell outcomes to identical weights, proportional to the smaller regional cell count; equal-cell weights give a sensitivity analysis. A second analysis adds reconstructor class and reports its support loss. Total variation of the joint cells, continuous-variable balance within bins and training complete-year balance are reported before and after weighting. Hidden-peak labels use withheld reference H30 and cannot be a deployment filter. No credible flow or regulation subset is available; unmatched\_gages is not an unregulated class. Regional and reconstructor associations cannot exclude process explanations.

Complete-year water--air H30 associations are reported both pooled and after centering each variable within station and period (before/from 2010). Paired resampling carries both periods of each connected group; station resampling is a sensitivity. These are annual associations between maxima that need not occur on the same dates, not daily gap-reconstruction coefficients.

Time-support controls reuse saved D2 annual predictions. Complete years remain in every scheme. We first retain the warm-season 90\% rule, its original one-draw random subset and its original spacing heuristic for incomplete years. A stronger exploratory comparison enumerates all 5,758 feasible same-count incomplete-year subsets in the 807 original common archives; 5,756 subsets belong to the 805 actually masked archives. Two additional choices use only calendar years and include the fixed complete set in their objective: minimizing the mean squared discrepancy between selected and full-reference empirical year CDFs on the integer-year grid, or maximizing centered year spread $S_{xx}$. Ties follow the other objective and then lexicographic incomplete-year order. These choices never inspect water temperature or reconstruction errors. Uniform random performance averages squared error over feasible subsets within each archive before taking the across-archive square root. Two hundred fixed random seeds separately describe selection variation; their percentile range is not a confidence interval. Paired 95\% percentile intervals resample the 17 connected groups 10,000 times, retaining every archive in the selected group. Station- and group-equal weighting are sensitivities. These exploratory intervals are pointwise summaries without multiplicity adjustment across controls and sensitivities. For each selected set, complete-year H30 gives year-set slope error $S$, annual estimates add reconstruction error $R$, and total error is $E=S+R$.

The historical classical comparator generates 20 residual-bridge paths. To evaluate budget sensitivity, we freeze the original six stations, 27 scored target station-years, masks, daily means and residual histories, and the original source interval-calibration roles and bootstrap multiplicities. The two target-region roles contribute 1,142 and 1,228 positive-weight calibration cases, 2,370 in total, at 19 stations in 102 station-years before 2010. Each role has four positive-weight connected groups, disjoint from its target group. Native 20-path generation exactly reproduces both the original source repair and target draws and quantiles. Nested 20/100/500 samples are prefixes of a 500-path call for seeds 0, 1 and 2. Because the generator consumes random arrays by day, the native 20-path sample differs from that 500-path prefix; the historical native baseline and nested-budget contrasts are kept distinct.

For every budget and seed we compare three target distributions: no offset, the original fixed source offset, and an offset recomputed on the original source role at that budget. Source quantiles are projected onto the observed bound before weighted calibration, using the original 50-positive-case stratum fallback. Target quantiles are projected, offset, sorted and projected again. No target outcome enters calibration. D2 quantiles remain fixed. All new CRPS comparisons use 256 common stratified probability levels with the same normal-spacing tail continuation, so that scoring approximation is held constant. Coverage, absolute endpoint movements and scores are reported by region/reconstructor; the target sample has only two connected groups. Neither this experiment nor subsequent joint-rank sampling establishes infinite-path convergence or a general method ranking.

\subsection{Observed windows remain facts under a less restrictive screen}

At \RRecordStations{} of \RRecordDenominator{} eligible stations, \RRecordStationYears{} incomplete years contain an observed window above the maximum complete-year H30 (Figure~\ref{fig:observed-records}). The practical consequence depends on the rule. Table~\ref{tab:rule-accounting} accounts for all \CCEligibleYears{} eligible station-years. Of the 35 selected witnesses, \CCWarmWitnessAdmitted{} pass the warm-season criterion and \CCAnnualWitnessAdmitted{} pass 95\% annual coverage. Among the warm-season exclusions, only \CCWarmExcludedFacts{} exceed the largest reported window among the other rule-accepted years, by \CCWarmIncrementOne{} and \CCWarmIncrementTwo{} $^{\circ}$C. Relative to the warm-season rule, the additional observed facts are the two excluded increments of \CCWarmIncrementOne{} and \CCWarmIncrementTwo{}$^{\circ}$C; remaining incomplete years stay unknown as annual H30. The practical evidence is a gain over the simple plug-in, not a demonstrated universal need for D2.

\subsection{Information conditions associated with regional recovery failures}
Hidden-peak fractions are similar in Bavaria (\PRBavariaHiddenFrac\%) and Switzerland (\PRFoenHiddenFrac\%), but their reconstruction support differs (Figure~\ref{fig:region-recovery}; Table~\ref{tab:region-policy}). Bavarian air-ridge hidden-peak 90\% intervals cover \PRBavariaHiddenAirCov\% with bias \PRBavariaHiddenAirBias{}$^{\circ}$C; \PRBavariaHiddenAirAbove\% of references exceed their upper endpoint. Donor-ridge coverage is \PRBavariaHiddenDonorCov\% in Bavaria and \PRFoenHiddenDonorCov\% in Switzerland. Daily out-of-fold residual scales are \PRBavariaAirDailySd{}, \PRBavariaDonorDailySd{} and \PRFoenDonorDailySd{}$^{\circ}$C, respectively. These are conditional associations among different cases, not a controlled change of reconstructor at the same station.

Common geometric support alone has regional cell-frequency total variation \PRRGeometryRawTV{}. Identical min-count weights reduce that joint-cell distance to zero and yield coverage \PRRGeometryMinFoenCov\% in Switzerland versus \PRRGeometryMinBavariaCov\% in Bavaria, a \PRRGeometryMinGap{}-point difference (Table~\ref{tab:region-standardized}). Equal-cell weighting also retains a contrast. Thus a contrast remains after equalizing the frequencies of these coarse geometric cells. Adding reconstructor retains only \PRRPolicyBavariaSupported{}/\PRRPolicyBavariaTotal{} Bavarian and \PRRPolicyFoenSupported{}/\PRRPolicyFoenTotal{} Swiss cases. Their standardized coverage difference is \PRRPolicyMinGap{} points, but Switzerland supplies no air-ridge counterpart to the main Bavarian failure layer. Removing 85.4\% of Bavarian cases cannot estimate how much reconstructor choice explains the original regional gap. Training complete-year counts also remain strongly imbalanced after weighting (absolute standardized differences exceed 2.7). The cause of the regional difference remains unidentified; process explanations have not been excluded. Deleting one connected group and recomputing common support and weights leaves the geometric coverage contrast positive (12.39--17.77 points), but the reconstructor-conditioned contrast can reverse sign (minimum $-1.12$ points). This is an influence check over changing supported populations, not a causal contrast.

\subsection{Year choice matters at fixed counts}
On \PTRTimeN{} actually masked forward controlled-gap archives, the warm-season rule retains \PRTimeWarmYears{} years on average and yields estimated-slope RMSE \PTRTimeWarmRMSE{}$^{\circ}$C per decade. Its original one-draw random and incomplete-year-only spacing controls give 0.4540 and 0.4566. Exact uniform averaging over all feasible subsets gives \PETimeUniformRMSE{}, with a paired group-resampling interval for its difference from warm-season RMSE spanning zero. This removes single-selection Monte Carlo noise from that comparison. The mean RMSE across 200 separately drawn selections is 0.4505 and their 2.5--97.5\% range is 0.4359--0.4671; these describe conditional selection variation, not uncertainty across independent rivers.

The stronger calendar controls improve performance (Figure~\ref{fig:time-support}; Table~\ref{tab:time-support}). Optimizing the empirical year CDF including fixed complete years gives RMSE \PETimeCDFRMSE{}, with a paired difference interval [\PETimeCDFLow, \PETimeCDFHigh]; maximizing $S_{xx}$ gives \PETimeSxxRMSE{} and [\PETimeSxxLow, \PETimeSxxHigh]. They change 367 and 376 warm-season selections, respectively. Both directions persist with station- or group-equal weights and after each of the 17 group deletions. These exploratory findings show that the earlier weak controls cannot support a blanket claim of no advantage for same-count selection. They assess calendar-based selection rules at fixed count; they neither establish equivalence nor separately identify the effect of adding years.

The count constraints force a single feasible set in \PTRTimeIdenticalN{}/\PTRTimeN{} archives: \PTRTimeNoneKeptN{} retain no incomplete year and \PTRTimeAllKeptN{} retain all. The other \PTRTimeChoiceN{} archives offer a choice. On this subset, warm-season, CDF and $S_{xx}$ RMSEs are 0.3944, 0.3594 and 0.3031. Retaining all supported years remains closer to the specified complete-record slope, with RMSE \PTRTimeAllRMSE{} on all 805 common masked archives, versus 0.4023 for the best tested same-count control. The paired all-minus-warm-season interval is $[-0.3557,-0.1841]$ $^{\circ}$C per decade.

Year-set RMSE under warm-season screening is \PRTimeWarmTruthRMSE{}, compared with reconstruction RMSE \PRTimeWarmReconRMSE{} on that same set. The all-year truth-set error is zero by definition; its estimated RMSE supplies the nontrivial recovery evidence. The signed identity
\[
\operatorname{MSE}(E)=\operatorname{MSE}(S)+\operatorname{MSE}(R)+2\operatorname{mean}(SR)
\]
includes error cancellation. For the original random control, $0.206128=0.208715+0.010617-0.013204$. Neither RMSE subtraction nor normalized squared terms identify independent causal contributions.

The original 807-archive paired cohort additionally contains two zero-mask archives, Bavarian stations 10081004/replicate 6 and 11405000/replicate 14, whose empty prediction schemas caused exclusion from the older diagnostic. Restoring their zero errors is a reported sensitivity. The remaining 13 of 820 archives retain only three or four warm-season years and fail the original five-year slope criterion. Their all-year RMSE is 0.3390 and the all-820 all-year RMSE is 0.1735; these unpaired availability results are not substituted for the 805-archive contrast.

\section{Discussion}
The contribution links observed-window facts, recovery of an annual statistic under stated information conditions, and a slope for a fixed record period. Direct window facts require no imputation. Year selection and annual reconstruction contribute distinct, potentially cancelling errors to the slope. The exact same-count experiment now shows that calendar positions can improve the result even without adding years. Restoring all supported years improves it further in the evaluated archives. This supports attention to time support without assigning a pure causal percentage to year count or treating near point estimates from weak controls as equivalence. The target remains the finite complete-record slope, not an identified climate-process trend.

A regional contrast remains after assigning common weights to the checked geometric cells. History, provider, region and reconstruction support remain entangled, however, and the absence of a Swiss air-ridge counterpart prevents causal attribution. The reconstructor-conditioned contrast can change sign when a group is removed. Similar within-station annual water--air slopes also conceal sensitivity to group and station weighting and do not establish stable gap-day residuals or peak timing.

The completed source-calibration experiment shows why the earlier pooled 0.007$^{\circ}$C diagnostic was insufficient. Recalibrated endpoints change in both target strata and local method comparisons depend on the Monte Carlo realization and budget. Historical native20 and a nested500 prefix are different random samples and remain explicitly distinguished. More paths can reduce sampling error without removing structural model or calibration error; 500 paths on two target groups do not prove convergence or transportability. The observation-only facts and D2 year-set comparisons do not depend on declaring a classical-method winner.

The cohort remains the long-complete tail of the archive: median 31 complete years versus 8 among the remaining 539 source stations, with 24 of 42 evaluation stations in Bavaria. Repeated masks and 18 connected groups do not constitute independent hydroclimates. Empirical calendars are closer to observed annual longest-gap conditions than controlled 30--90-day stresses, while actual same-day co-missingness is much lower on the recorded peer-conditioned denominator. That comparison does not measure every model donor's availability or a common outage cause. External-reference limitations and the negative release and matched-heat-stress results remain in Supporting Information. All added diagnostics are exploratory rather than independent confirmation.

\section{Conclusions}
Observed 30-day windows in incomplete years can overturn a complete-year heat record. The 19 warm-season-accepted windows add facts relative to strict completeness; two excluded windows add facts relative to the warm-season rule. In the controlled-gap experiment, stronger calendar-based selections improve finite-record slope recovery at fixed year counts, and restoring all supported years improves it further. Regional standardization retains a geometric contrast without identifying its cause. Repeating both source and target trajectory sampling and recalibrating on the original source roles changes endpoints and local method comparisons, so a small pooled change cannot certify numerical stability. Preserve direct heat facts, specify the record-period target and year-selection rule, and assess reconstruction, source calibration and numerical sampling together.
