# Cover letter — Water Resources Research

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, “Fitting-period error curves outperform
structural risk estimates for stream-temperature gaps,” for publication in
*Water Resources Research*.

The operational question is simple: before a monitoring agency fills a long
temperature outage or changes a station network, what evidence predicts the
loss that its recovery pipeline will incur? We connect three literatures that
usually remain separate—artificial-gap stress testing, cross-network
regionalization, and monitoring-network design—and test the connection on 55
open-development river networks and a first panel of 42 new networks from
North America and Europe.

The main result is a correction to our original premise. A four-coalition
conditional-covariance operator added little beyond simple descriptors, and
its risk saturated as gaps lengthened. Seasonally stratified recovery errors
measured wholly inside fitting years were substantially more predictive of
later loss. This negative method result and positive empirical alternative are
reported together. We also show which parts generalize across three recovery
families and which do not: ranking is robust, while magnitude calibration,
whole-network coverage, and finite-sample safe-fill control remain domain
dependent.

The manuscript does not claim a new Gaussian sensor-design objective,
reservoir causation, operational safe filling, or authorization to remove
stations. Real-data placement replay and labelled domain-adaptation curves are
identified as post-confirmation method development. A second independent
confirmation is registered separately and has not been represented as
complete.
