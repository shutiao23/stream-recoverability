# Cover letter — Water Resources Research

Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, “Fitting-period error curves improve
network-level risk ranking for stream-temperature gaps,” for publication in
*Water Resources Research*.

The operational question is simple: before a monitoring agency fills a long
temperature outage or changes a station network, what evidence predicts the
loss that its recovery pipeline will incur? We connect three literatures that
usually remain separate—artificial-gap stress testing, cross-network
regionalization, and monitoring-network design—and test the connection on 55
development river networks and a first panel of 42 new networks from
North America and Europe.

The main result is a correction to our original premise. Seasonally stratified
recovery errors measured wholly inside fitting years were the strongest tested
predictor of later loss. The first-panel method-development analysis reached
network-level Spearman 0.922 on four supported horizons; the second evaluation
reproduced the finding at 0.805 across 57 scored networks. Across the complete
first-panel grid, a network-mean fallback retained
the network-level rank advantage but lost pooled-rank and magnitude performance;
this fallback cost is reported as a main boundary rather than hidden as missing
support. A four-coalition conditional-covariance operator added little beyond
simpler predictors because its risk saturated as gaps lengthened.
This empirical alternative and the negative analytic
result are reported together. We also show the decision boundary: ordering
persists across three statistical recovery families but is weak in a bounded
six-network GRU-style sensitivity and a development-only
air-temperature--flow proxy. The latter is not published air2stream and lacks
confirmation inputs. Magnitude calibration, whole-network coverage,
finite-sample safe-fill control, and station-placement benefit also do not
transfer as operational guarantees.

Artificial-gap stress testing is established; our contribution is its
whole-network transfer test, the observed saturation mechanism, and the
cross-domain decision boundary. The manuscript does not claim a new Gaussian
sensor-design objective, reservoir causation, operational safe filling, or a
validated basis for removing stations. First-panel real-data placement replay
and labelled domain-adaptation curves are identified as
post-confirmation method development. The second-confirmation v2 amendment was internally frozen and
hash-bound to the exact roster before recovery scoring, but it was not placed
in a separate public commit before the outcomes; we report that provenance
limitation explicitly.

Under that amendment, 57 of 60 attempted networks were scoreable. Directly
supported empirical horizons reproduced strong station-gap and network-level
ordering with near-unit calibration, and the complete-panel empirical
predictor retained higher network-level rank than the simple model. The
decision layer still failed: network-block intervals were more than eight
times median loss in width, and exact 5% triage certified and released no
units. Placement regret was directionally 6.0% lower than random, but the
protocol lacked a utility margin. The manuscript presents these results as
replication of ranking and calibration, not closure of the management endpoint.
