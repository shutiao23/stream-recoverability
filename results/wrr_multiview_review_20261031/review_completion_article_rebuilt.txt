12   Abstract

13   Screening incomplete river-temperature years changes the time support of sus-
14   tained heat. We examine observed heat windows, finite-record slopes of the annual
15   maximum 30-day mean (H30), and information conditions associated with re-
16   covery failures. Whole-archive evaluation uses 42 long complete records from a

17   581-station source archive. Observed windows in incomplete years can exceed every
18   complete-year heat maximum; most witnesses also pass a warm-season rule. On
19   805 forward controlled-gap archives, the median complete-record slope is 0.75◦ C

20   per decade. Recovering all supported years gives slope RMSE 0.170, versus 0.446
21   under warm-season screening, so the screening error is more than half the median
22   slope. Calendar-position controls that include the fixed complete years reduce
23   RMSE to 0.428 and 0.402; their paired intervals exclude zero. Weak same-count

24   controls do not. Hidden-peak 90% coverage is 67.9% for Bavarian air-ridge recon-
25   structions. Common geometric weighting retains a regional contrast, but support
26   loss and training-history imbalance prevent causal attribution. A trajectory-budget

27   experiment regenerates 2,370 original source-calibration cases and scores the same
28   27 target station-years. Across three seeds, source-recalibrated median absolute
29   95th-percentile changes from 20 to 500 nested paths are 0.069–0.117◦ C in the Swiss
30   subset and 0.061–0.101◦ C in Bavaria. Five hundred paths do not establish conver-

31   gence or a preferred estimator. These results support preserving observed heat facts
32   and evaluating year selection, reconstruction and numerical uncertainty for a stated
33   period.


34   Plain Language Summary
35   Gaps in a river’s temperature record do not necessarily erase a hot year: thirty observed

36   days can already be warmer than every complete year. Most such examples also pass a
37   warm-season screen. In our experiments, the choice of years matters even when the
38   number retained is fixed. Choosing years that better cover the record period reduces slope

39   error, while restoring all supported years improves it further. Recovery failures
40   concentrate in Bavarian air-supported reconstructions, but regional comparisons cannot
41   identify their cause. More simulated paths and recalibration on the original independent
42   source data change uncertainty endpoints and some method comparisons. These findings

43   favor retaining observed heat facts and stating the temporal and information support of
44   any reconstructed summary.

45   1 Introduction

46        Stream-temperature records support studies of thermal habitat, climate change
47   and water management (Caissie, 2006; Michel et al., 2020; van Hamel & Brunner,
48   2024). The annual maximum of a 30-day mean (H30) summarizes sustained heat

49   and is related to the thermal peak used in river-network studies (Beaufort et al.,
50   2022). A monitoring gap can change this statistic through the years it removes,
51   the thermal states it hides, and the information that remains from other years or
52   stations.

53        Prior work has already shown that partial stream-temperature records can
54   support some annual analyses. Johnson et al. (2021) evaluated missing intervals and
55   imputation for annual mean, phase and amplitude, finding limitations that varied

56   among rivers. Beaufort et al. (2022) used summer observations and climate correc-
57   tion for a multiyear thermal-peak metric. Those results concern different targets:
58   Johnson and coauthors do not supply a universal error baseline for annual H30, and

59   Beaufort and coauthors do not evaluate jointly degraded multiyear archives. The
60   question added here is how completeness screening changes the time support of
61   sustained-heat information, and which observations and river-state information then
62   leave annual recovery and trend propagation without support.

63        That question has two mechanisms. First, the years that a rule deletes, and
64   the thermal states and time-leverage those years carry, change a slope fitted to
65   the remaining finite record. Second, river thermal response, data processing and

66   source support jointly determine the error of recovering a heat peak inside a gap.
67   The intended use is a comparable finite-record H30 slope on a stated record period,
68   together with fully observed 30-day windows retained as archive facts. If an observed
69   lower bound already exceeds a pre-defined H30 threshold, that exceedance can be

70   kept without a precise annual value. Study thresholds are not ecological-hazard
71   standards, and a 0.5◦ C reconstruction-error flag is not a calibrated probability at
72   another threshold.

73        Regression record augmentation and maintenance-of-variance methods have
74   a long hydrological history (Matalas & Jacobs, 1964; Hirsch, 1982; Vogel & Ste-
75   dinger, 1985). A point prediction, a predictive distribution and a decision to release
76   a value have different targets. Distributional imputation should be assessed for

77    the downstream quantity rather than only for daily squared error (Näf et al., 2023;

78    Gneiting & Raftery, 2007). We compare a daily plug-in, simple corrections, source-
79    role-calibrated stochastic reconstruction and annual distribution regression, then
80    jointly hide portions of a station’s multiyear record before local refitting.

83         We organize the evidence around three research questions. RQ1: what ob-
84    served heat information does each completeness rule retain or discard? RQ2: how
85    do the retained year set and annual reconstruction errors combine in a slope for a
86    specified record period? RQ3: under which visible information conditions do hidden-

87    peak recovery errors concentrate? The regional, same-count and trajectory-budget
88    diagnostics were developed after inspecting existing results; they are exploratory
89    rather than preregistered confirmation. The six-station trajectory subset was fixed

90    before that replay, not before the wider study. Its 0.05◦ C tolerance is a numerical
91    diagnostic, not a hydrological acceptance threshold. Table 1 states the corresponding
92    evidence, denominators and boundaries. Throughout, calibrated means an empirical
93    offset estimated on disjoint source-role groups; it does not mean that a target region

94    attains nominal coverage.

211        3.2 Whole-archive degradation, common cohorts and task-level

212                 uncertainty

213        The whole-archive benchmark jointly masks selected gaps in several years of
214   a target station before fitting its daily models, climatology or residual histories.

215   Unmasked target observations and neighboring physical stations remain available in
216   this experiment. The benchmark contains 42 stations with at least twenty complete
217   reference years in seven regions. One Finnish record ends in 1994, leaving 41 stations
218   for forward evaluation. Twenty paired mask repetitions use both empirical calendars

219   and controlled 30–90-day gaps; after seven observed burn-in years, each reference
220   year is degraded with probability 0.35. These 3,320 archive experiments do not reuse
221   any masked target day when fitting another year. The complete reference, including

222   those hidden days, is used for scoring; its unmasked values can also enter local fit-
223   ting. The archived single-year plug-in is retained as an optimistic-information paired
224   comparator on identical masks.

225        Offline daily fits use the remaining visible history. Forward annual models and
226   interval offsets use source observations before 2010 and exclude the evaluated test
227   stations and their connected groups. The original partitioned role assignment can re-
228   tain other training-only groups in the same named region: this occurs in the HUC17

229   estimator and in NVE interval calibration. Accordingly this forward experiment
230   is held-test-group, not uniformly held-region, and is applied to 2010 onward. The
231   strict-region sensitivity removes the additional same-region groups and is reported
232   separately. Forward local daily fitting and dependence estimation also exclude water

233   observations from 2010 onward. These are current-vintage retrospective forward
234   experiments, not a historical real-time field trial. Annual regression weights remain
235   source-only and use source-fit seed zero throughout. Source observations do not

236   change across archive masks, so those fits are reused; every local stage that uses the
237   degraded target archive is refitted. Twenty archive masks are not twenty new global
238   model fits.

239        On each degraded archive, the retention set (90% warm-season rule or all sup-
240   ported years) is crossed with the annual estimator (daily plug-in, equally calibrated
241   stochastic regression or D2). Every slope is scored against the same complete-record
242   slope. For the four MSE cells A (rule/plug-in), B (all/plug-in), C (rule/alternative)

250           3.3 Specification of the executed estimators

251           Daily completion uses one recorded reconstructor per case: donor ridge,
252   air ridge, linear interpolation, smooth climatology, or day-of-year climatology.

253   Air2stream-3 and the value computed from observed days are additional compara-
254   tors. A reconstructor may use days that remain visible in the target year, the
255   station’s other visible years, and eligible neighboring stations. Days hidden by that
256   year’s target mask are not used to fit the local model. In the forward experiment,

257   local daily fitting also excludes the target station’s water observations from 2010
258   onward.

259           D2 learns the annual H30 error relative to the plug-in, not the annual value

260   itself. Seven LightGBM quantile regressions use the quantile objective at levels 0.05,
261   0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 0.90 and 0.95. The fixed settings, which were not re-tuned for
262   this revision, are 300 trees, learning rate 0.05, 31 leaves, at least 50 cases per leaf,
263   80% row and column subsampling, and an L2 penalty of 1. Features are available at

264   decision time: gap length and runs, warm-season and peak overlap, donor count and
265   correlation, historical reconstruction error, the visible edge anomaly, air anomalies,
266   differences among the other reconstructors relative to the plug-in, and summaries

267   of the stochastic and air-state simulation distributions when those summaries exist.
268   A missing simulation feature stays missing and uses the tree’s missing-value branch.
269   Predicted quantiles are sorted to remove crossings. The published annual point is
270   the calibrated 0.50 quantile.

271           The historical calibrated stochastic comparator stores 20 residual-bridge trajec-
272   tories as a fixed computational budget. The same-target experiment reported below
273   regenerates the original source-calibration cases at larger budgets and updates their




                                                    –11–
                              manuscript submitted to Water Resources Research



274   offsets; it does not rerun the full whole-archive trend comparison. Fine method rank-

275   ings are not asserted beyond the tested numerical subset. The bridge is a Gaussian
276   AR(1) on a residual history with at least 30 finite days. Its lag coefficient is clipped
277   to [−0.95, 0.995]. When an observed water value exists at the left or right end of
278   a gap, the trajectory is conditioned on that boundary; otherwise the left state is

279   drawn from the residual margin. The same seven probability levels are read from
280   those 20 trajectories. Where a stored bridge-repair table applies, it replaces the
281   stochastic columns. It does not refit the daily bridge.

282        Both annual distributions use four disjoint connected-group roles, with shares
283   0.55, 0.15, 0.15 and 0.15: estimator fitting, interval calibration, score learning and
284   rule calibration. Groups are shuffled within the source seed and bootstrapped

285   only inside their own role. Each role must contain at least two groups. Interval
286   offsets are empirical quantiles of the calibration-role errors, computed separately by
287   peak-overlap bin and reconstructor. A stratum with fewer than 50 positive-weight
288   calibration cases falls back to all calibration-role cases. Levels at or above 0.50 use

289   the higher empirical quantile, and lower levels use the lower one. Offsets are added
290   and the seven levels are sorted again.

291        Sampling then follows one order. A finite observed-window lower bound is

292   applied to the annual quantiles. Stratified probabilities are interpolated between the
293   seven stored levels. Outside 0.05 and 0.95, the default extends the quantile function
294   by the adjacent normal-quantile spacing; holding the endpoint fixed is retained
295   only as a paired sensitivity. These seven levels do not identify a complete central

296   90% distribution: values between them are interpolations, and both outer tails are
297   extrapolations. Samples are then projected onto the same lower bound. Independent
298   ranks, auxiliary Ridge year-bootstrap states, or three-year residual blocks reorder

299   the samples without changing each year’s marginal. The ordinary-least-squares slope
300   and its interval are computed from that joint sample. Task-level calibration is a
301   later, separate step.

302        We refit both methods’ annual quantile offsets on their original disjoint source
303   calibration groups after imposing the observed-window lower bound. Both distri-
304   butions and their samples respect the bound when one is available. Seven stored
305   quantiles constrain only those seven probability levels; they do not identify the




                                                   –12–
                            manuscript submitted to Water Resources Research



306   distribution between or outside them. We extrapolate both outer tails using the ad-

307   jacent normal-quantile spacing and project the annual samples onto the bound; the
308   endpoint-constant alternative is retained on paired cases. The normal extrapolation
309   is an assumption, not a validated tail law. That projection is applied to the annual
310   distribution and then carried into the slope. It does not project a trend interval

311   onto an annual lower bound. The main analysis uses 256 stratified samples per year.
312   Within one estimator, independent ranks, auxiliary shared year-bootstrap regres-
313   sion states and three-year residual blocks keep that estimator’s annual marginals

314   fixed. The two estimators do not share one annual distribution. Shared states come
315   from a small harmonic/air Ridge model, not the D2 tree posterior or the selected
316   donor model. These couplings are dependence sensitivities, not posterior samples or
317   coverage theorems.

318        The joint-rank numerical sensitivity fixes the annual quantiles, observed
319   bounds and evaluation years and increases the stratified sample count through 256,
320   1,024 and 4,096 on the difficult forward controlled gaps. Three random seeds index

321   the rank construction. The rank sequence is generated at 4,096 draws and truncated
322   for smaller counts; annual marginals are regenerated separately for each sample
323   size. We report coverage, width, interval score, the three-seed mean and the largest

324   single-seed change. A 0.3-point rule on the three-seed mean is only a diagnostic:
325   averaging seeds can cancel opposing changes, and meeting it does not justify ranking
326   small construction differences. The check uses the same opened data and is not an
327   independent validation.

752   5 Discussion

753        The contribution links observed-window facts, recovery of an annual statistic
754   under stated information conditions, and a slope for a fixed record period. Direct
755   window facts require no imputation. Year selection and annual reconstruction con-

756   tribute distinct, potentially cancelling errors to the slope. The exact same-count
757   experiment now shows that calendar positions can improve the result even without
758   adding years. Restoring all supported years improves it further in the evaluated
759   archives. This supports attention to time support without assigning a pure causal

760   percentage to year count or treating near point estimates from weak controls as
761   equivalence. The target remains the finite complete-record slope, not an identified
762   climate-process trend.

763        A regional contrast remains after assigning common weights to the checked
764   geometric cells. History, provider, region and reconstruction support remain en-
765   tangled, however, and the absence of a Swiss air-ridge counterpart prevents causal

766   attribution. The reconstructor-conditioned contrast can change sign when a group is
767   removed. Similar within-station annual water–air slopes also conceal sensitivity to
768   group and station weighting and do not establish stable gap-day residuals or peak
769   timing.

770        The completed source-calibration experiment shows why the earlier pooled
771   0.007◦ C diagnostic was insufficient. Recalibrated endpoints change in both target
772   strata and local method comparisons depend on the Monte Carlo realization and

773   budget. Historical native20 and a nested500 prefix are different random samples and
774   remain explicitly distinguished. More paths can reduce sampling error without re-
775   moving structural model or calibration error; 500 paths on two target groups do not
776   prove convergence or transportability. The observation-only facts and D2 year-set

777   comparisons do not depend on declaring a classical-method winner.

778        The cohort remains the long-complete tail of the archive: median 31 complete
779   years versus 8 among the remaining 539 source stations, with 24 of 42 evaluation

780   stations in Bavaria. Repeated masks and 18 connected groups do not constitute
781   independent hydroclimates. Empirical calendars are closer to observed annual
782   longest-gap conditions than controlled 30–90-day stresses, while actual same-day
783   co-missingness is much lower on the recorded peer-conditioned denominator. That



                                                 –26–
                                manuscript submitted to Water Resources Research



784   comparison does not measure every model donor’s availability or a common out-

785   age cause. External-reference limitations and the negative release and matched-
786   heat-stress results remain in Supporting Information. All added diagnostics are
787   exploratory rather than independent confirmation.


788   6 Conclusions

789        Observed 30-day windows in incomplete years can overturn a complete-year
790   heat record. The 19 warm-season-accepted windows add facts relative to strict com-

791   pleteness; two excluded windows add facts relative to the warm-season rule. In the
792   controlled-gap experiment, the median complete-record slope is 0.754◦ C per decade.
793   Warm-season screening error is more than half of that median, and restoring all sup-

794   ported years brings the error below one quarter of it. Fixed-count rules that place
795   years, including the complete years, can improve on warm-season screening; match-
796   ing the year count alone does not. Regional standardization retains a geometric
797   contrast without identifying its cause. Repeating both source and target trajectory

798   sampling and recalibrating on the original source roles changes endpoints and local
799   method comparisons, so a small pooled change cannot certify numerical stability.
800   Preserve direct heat facts, specify the record-period target and year-selection rule,

801   and assess reconstruction, source calibration and numerical sampling together.
