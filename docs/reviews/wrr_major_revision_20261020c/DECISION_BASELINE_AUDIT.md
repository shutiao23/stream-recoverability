# Post-test deterministic decision baselines

The original H3 comparator and complete cost grid remain unchanged. In the
scientific audit after the frozen test was scored, add always-unknown,
always-hot and always-cool reference policies. These require no model fitting,
new data, threshold or outcome-based policy choice. Their costs are arithmetic
consequences of the already declared loss and reference labels.

For N original candidates, the three losses are respectively c_A,
n_cool/N and c_FN*n_hot/N. Always unknown produces no magnitude estimate and
does not have a finite selected risk. Reporting it as risk zero would be wrong.

At the displayed Swiss retrospective FN5/A0.05 example, the screen's absolute
loss is about 0.1191, versus 0.05 for always unknown. Its large improvement over
reconstructing all does not establish operational desirability. Other
predeclared sensitivity cells can outperform both at their point estimates,
but none is promoted after testing to a new confirmatory primary endpoint.
Full values are in SI Table S23's source CSV. No model or threshold lock changes.

This is an adversarial strengthening of the comparison, not a tuning step or
a way to rescue a failed representation hypothesis. A new minimum-coverage
constraint would require a new prospective decision specification.
