UNSENT DRAFT — SUBJECT TO AUTHOR REVIEW

Subject: Clarification of verified hourly water-temperature data and time basis

Dear Nova Scotia Environment and Climate Change water monitoring team,

We are assessing the reliability of annual sustained river-temperature statistics when monitoring records have gaps. We are investigating the department's historical verified workbooks and the Open Data Portal continuous water-quality dataset (bkfi-mjgw) for Kelley, LaHave, North East Margaree and Shelburne rivers.

Before treating any records as a high-quality reference, could you clarify:

1. For the 2009–2017 verified workbooks, what does “verified” mean specifically for temperature, and are interpolated or estimated values present? Are there point-level or time-interval quality flags, corrections, sensor calibration or field-verification records available for these years?
   We found the department's 2010 network report describing segment-level ratings and linear adjustments for the earlier record, but cannot tell whether the same procedures, rating thresholds, and omission rules apply to 2009–2017.
2. What time basis applies to each workbook and to the Open Data Portal date/time fields? The LaHave workbook header states UTC-04:00; does this represent a fixed offset throughout the record, or local civil time with daylight-saving transitions? What applies at the other stations?
3. Are each reported hourly temperature an instantaneous hourly observation or a mean of shorter-interval readings? If a mean, what minimum within-hour sampling or quality is required?
4. Do the historical verified workbooks and current Open Data Portal represent different revisions or processing? In an exploratory comparison, some matching reported date/time labels have different temperature values, including differences above 0.5 °C. We have not assumed that either release is incorrect. Can you identify the correct provenance, corrections and intended quality interpretation for the two releases?
5. Can the historical workbooks or equivalent verified observations be shared with journal reviewers or deposited under the same Open Government Licence – Nova Scotia identified by the Open Data Portal, with attribution? If not, what reviewer access method and citation would you recommend?

The candidate years were screened only for the presence of reported hourly labels and finite numeric cells. No reconstruction performance was evaluated from these data, and we recognize that a numeric value is not proof of a nonestimated or error-free observation.

Thank you for considering these questions.
