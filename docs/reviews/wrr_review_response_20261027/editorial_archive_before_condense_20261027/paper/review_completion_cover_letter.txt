Dear Editors of Water Resources Research,

Please consider our manuscript, “Incomplete river-temperature years: separating recovery, screening and trend inference.” The study examines how incomplete daily records affect sustained river heat extremes and the slopes calculated from a finite multiyear archive.

Observed 30-day windows establish that strict complete-year selection can miss reported heat records. Most of these witnesses pass a less restrictive warm-season rule, which specifies the practical scope of that observation. Across a multi-region source archive, a calibrated classical reconstruction performs similarly to annual distribution regression. Jointly degraded station archives show that retaining years can improve the evaluated finite-record slope, while later-year trend intervals remain insufficiently covered under the studied assumptions.

We separately assess numerical release, observed lower bounds and unknown cases. The resulting evidence identifies limits of group-risk control and sensor-quality detection rather than claiming an operational safety guarantee. Additional reference intakes and their qualification failures are reported transparently; they do not establish a new independent accuracy validation.
